## Situation

On August 25, 2026, Aaron adopted h1_v132_mortgate (density-dependent mortality scaling gated on the managed flag) as the new default configuration for FVS-ACD across all eleven benchmarked GYDB sources (pass 31, commit 10ea8418), because it never lost to plain v13.2 on RMSE against short-interval pair-validation data. A Bakuzis biological-consistency assessment (Leary 1997), begun immediately after to check the same configuration over a full rotation rather than a short remeasurement interval, was interrupted mid-run. An 11-task SLURM array (job 13965767) had already completed, producing trajectory CSVs and check-script output for all eleven sources at `~/magplot/verify/bakuzis_h1/` on Cardinal, but two per-source memos were lost with the interrupted session (the underlying Cardinal output was not lost, only the memo text) and the headline finding, a mid-rotation basal area and volume crash inverting standing-volume site ordering at nearly every source except PEF, was reported by a subagent whose work was interrupted before verification. This pass (32) re-verifies that finding from primary data, closes the one analysis gap the interrupted run left open (no plain-v13.2 comparison existed), and issues a verdict on whether pass 31's adoption decision should stand, be caveated, or be reversed.

## ORIENT

**Statistical and compute vulnerabilities.** Each source's trajectory rests on 1 to 4 real stands, not a resampled or bootstrapped population, so the Reineke and Eichhorn tests are underpowered at several sources and their FLAG or OK read should not be over-interpreted at the per-source level; the volume site-ordering test is the most robust of the four relations tested here because it only requires within-source rank comparison across ages, not a slope fit or a cross-source CV. No parallel or thread-safety concerns apply, since each SLURM array task runs one source independently.

**Blind spot pass.**

1. What would embarrass this if a reviewer found it first? Two things, caught and corrected before this report was finalized rather than after. First, Baxter's vacuous clean pass, which the interrupted run's own framing implicitly leaned on ("except PEF"). Second, medev's 3/100 volume-inversion count, which looks like a second near-clean source at first glance but carries 11 total flags including a severe Eichhorn violation (41.4 percent CV) and 55/100 height inversions, so it is not a clean source overall and is reported with that caveat rather than folded into an "except PEF and medev" headline that would overstate the exception.

2. What is the standard approach not proposed here, and why is the chosen approach defensible? A full refit or re-parameterization of the ingrowth-release gating (conditioning recruitment on canopy or volume recovery rather than density alone) would be the standard fix once a submodel defect is this well localized. It is not attempted in this pass, which is a falsification and diagnosis tool by design (per the bakuzis-assessment skill's own stated scope), not a refitting pass; recommending the fix and scoping it as follow-up work is the defensible substitute.

3. What is the ceiling? A model that passed every Bakuzis relation at every source, including a genuine multi-site pass at all eleven, every Reineke slope near -1.605, and a low Eichhorn CV everywhere, would be the ceiling. No model in this repository's history has reached that ceiling (the single-cohort CONUS assessment referenced in `20260625_singlecohort_bakuzis_assessment.md` shows even the cleanest arm tested there, the legacy engine, failing the pooled Eichhorn on real height and 0/4 in one region). FVS-ACD v13.2 with or without h1, at 1 of 11 sources clean on volume ordering and 10 of 11 showing the crash to varying degree, is well below this ceiling but is not an outlier against the workspace's own prior Bakuzis results, which is a useful plausibility anchor rather than a passing grade.

4. What single input, if wrong, invalidates everything downstream? Whether `V1211_MORTGATE` genuinely defaults to FALSE and `ops$mort.dens.scale` genuinely defaults to OFF in `AcadianGY_13.1.r`. This is checked directly (line 4137's own comment, plus the runner script's env-var default), not assumed, since the entire baseline-versus-h1 isolation depends on this default being real and not itself a bug.

5. Which inputs were built for a different extent, population, or period than this run targets? The pair-validation data behind pass 24 through 31's RMSE adoption decision, built for and validated against 1- to 25-year remeasurement intervals, is being read here for a 100-year extrapolation question it was never built to answer. This is the F1 hunting question, and it is answered explicitly above rather than left as an unstated assumption: the crash is a finding ABOUT the extrapolation domain, not a defect IN the calibration domain.

## DECIDE

**Design.** Reuse `bakuzis_h1default_traj.R` verbatim in its model-sourcing, v13.2 overlay, and ops-assembly logic, since these blocks are shared production code, not part of the question under test. Produce `bakuzis_v132baseline_traj.R` by removing exactly the three lines that set `ops$mort.dens.scale`, `ops$mort.dens.fmin/k/rd0` (the h1-specific block), leaving `V1211_MORTGATE`'s coded default of FALSE in effect, so the baseline run is byte-identical to production-default plain v13.2 by construction, not by re-implementation. `[ASSUMPTION: the three-line removal is the correct and complete isolation of the h1 lever, since it is the only ops-object difference the merge_v24 runner's own V1211_MORTGATE gate controls; verified against AcadianGY_13.1.r's own default-OFF comment rather than assumed from the runner script alone]`.

**Gates.** Gate 1 (location axis), each of the 6 rerun sources' h1 versus baseline check-script report is compared source by source, not pooled, so a difference at one source cannot be masked by agreement at another; threshold, any FLAG-count difference greater than 1 (allowing for tie-breaking noise near a threshold age) triggers a closer look. All 6 sources passed this gate (largest observed FLAG-count difference: zero; largest HT-inversion-count difference: 3, at QC). Gate 2 (value-range axis), CTRN's age-by-age BA/VOL/TPH numeric comparison, threshold, any divergence exceeding 10 percent at the crash trough would indicate a materially different mechanism; observed divergence was 1 to 2 percent, provenance external (a direct arithmetic comparison of two CSVs, not anchored to any internal scorecard). Gate 3 (total-magnitude axis), the crash's onset relative density (0.55) compared against Long (1985)'s published self-thinning zone boundaries (imminent competition mortality onset at relative density 0.55 to 0.60), provenance external (published forestry literature, not this repository). Gate 4 (cross-layer consistency axis), the h1-versus-baseline agreement pattern (identical or near-identical at all 6 rerun sources, spanning the full severity range observed) checked for internal consistency, since a mechanism genuinely shared between configurations should produce this pattern at low-severity and high-severity sources alike, which it does (memat and NB, the two highest-flag-count sources, are byte-identical; MEFIA_L, a lower-severity source, is also identical).

## Verdict

Keep h1_v132_mortgate as the pass 31 default, with a documented long-horizon caveat that applies to plain v13.2 generally. The short-interval RMSE case for adoption (pass 24 through 31) is unaffected, since it was never in question here; the long-horizon Bakuzis finding is a real, disclosed limitation of FVS-ACD v13.2's ingrowth-release gating, present with or without the h1 lever, and should accompany any full-rotation or long-horizon use of either configuration until the gating is revised to condition recruitment on canopy or volume recovery rather than density alone.
