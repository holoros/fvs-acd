# Plan: hardwood form and risk modifiers for FVS-ACD

15 July 2026. A staged plan to test whether predicting hardwood form and risk, then applying form and risk keyed growth, mortality, and volume/value modifiers, improves FVS-ACD hardwood predictions. Grounded in data/hardwood_form_risk_investigation.md. No em dashes.

## Why this is the right lever

Hardwood is the shared error frontier in the frozen benchmark and the error is scatter, not bias. On the frozen basis v12.10.1 hardwood total volume reads about +19 percent mean bias but about 76 percent %RMSE, and value about +20 percent bias against about 81 percent %RMSE; OSM is the same order (about 75 percent %RMSE). Every model shares it, so the missing signal is a stand or tree property none of them uses, not a coefficient error. Hardwood merchantable volume and value depend heavily on stem form and defect or risk: two trees of the same species, diameter, and height can differ greatly in sound merchantable volume and in sawlog value depending on sweep, fork, multiple stems, and decay or damage risk. That is exactly the axis form and risk encode, which makes it the most plausible source of the hardwood scatter that diameter, height, and species alone cannot resolve.

## What already exists (do not rebuild)

The Castle form and risk machinery is already coded in the AcadianGY engine (verified in AcadianGY_13.0.r and the 12.6.2 prototype):

- calc_form_prob: assigns four NHRI form classes (single stem STM, sweep or lean LSW, multiple stems MST, fork LF) as normalized multinomial logistic probabilities from species and DBH, for red oak, sugar maple, yellow birch, and red maple.
- calc_risk_prob: high risk probability from species, DBH, and a species by DBH interaction, same four species.
- ddbh_hw_mod: a multiplicative diameter increment modifier keyed to a collapsed form (A or B) and risk (low or high), Castle 2018, species RO SM YB RM PB QA.
- tsurv_hw_mod: a survival modifier on the same keying, Castle 2018.

The blocking disconnect: the growth and survival modifiers are gated to fire only when observed form and risk are present in the tree list, behind ops$use.hw.mod. No benchmark input carries form or risk, so the modifier is inert in the frozen benchmark. The predicted probabilities from calc_form_prob and calc_risk_prob are computed but never wired into the modifier path. Wiring the prediction into the modifier is the immediate lever. Separately, an older hw.saw.prop sawlog proportion function (form and risk keyed) existed in the 12.6.2 protos but is absent from v13.0, so value has no form or risk term at all, which is likely where the hardwood value error concentrates.

## Data reality

Form and risk are observed nowhere on Cardinal. FIA ME_TREE has FORMCL populated 0.0 percent and TREE_GRADE 0.4 percent; the OSM and MagPlot treelists carry Grade, Risk, CR, CW columns but they are unpopulated. So form and risk can only be model assigned for the benchmark, which is the point of the prediction functions. Observed proxies do exist in FIA (TREECLCD 91 percent, crown ratio 77 percent, CULL 52 percent, damage agent 22 to 24 percent, decay 6 percent) and MagPlot condition codes, useful later for validation and for enriching predictors. Validating the predicted form and risk themselves needs the Castle 2017 and Smith 2022 raw field data, which must be sourced.

## Literature anchor

Castle et al. 2017 (CJFR 47:1457-1467, form and risk prediction and sawlog volume for four hardwoods), Castle et al. 2018 (CJFR 48, form and damage effects on diameter increment and survival, the modifier coefficients), Castle 2017 MS thesis, Smith 2022 MS thesis (form and risk class distributions and sawlog product ratios, 14 species, the natural extension to more species), and the parallel NHRI work (Bennemann et al. 2023, Moreau et al. 2023 2024).

## Ryan Smith thesis (2022): what it adds

Full reference: Smith, R. 2022. Form and risk class distribution and its effect on estimated product ratios in commercial species commonly found in northeastern hardwood forests and a comparative analysis of contrasting field inventory methodologies. MS thesis (Forest Resources), University of Maine, December 2022, advisor A. Weiskittel. Two chapters. A copy is archived for the record at repos/fvs-acd/refs/Ryan_Smith_Thesis_2022.pdf.

What Smith modeled. Smith took the same NHRI form and risk scheme Castle used (Pelletier et al. 2016b, the New Brunswick tree classification: eight form classes F1 to F8 and four risk classes R1 to R4) and applied it to a much larger and broader sample, 18,003 trees on 1,794 plots across Maine, drawn from four sources measured by five cruising teams. He consolidated the scheme differently from the Castle engine: form collapses to a binary ideal form (F1) versus acceptable form (F2 to F8), and risk collapses to three groups (R1, R2, and R3 where R3 folds in R4). The Castle code instead uses four form classes (STM, LSW, MST, LF) and two risk levels, so the two consolidations are not identical. Smith split the sample into three species groups (hardwoods, spruce-fir, other softwoods) and built three families of models for each: form and risk prediction models, sawlog presence models, and product ratio models (the proportion of merchantable volume that is sawlog quality). This is the same two-step presence-then-proportion structure Castle used, with the product ratio the piece the current v13.0 engine is missing.

Species coverage, the Phase 3 basis. The hardwood models cover eight named species plus an other-hardwood pool: red oak, sugar maple, yellow birch, and red maple (Castle's four) plus American beech, paper birch, quaking aspen, and white ash (the four additions), with species entering as a fixed effect and a species-by-DBH interaction (Table 1.5, Table A.9). Those four additions are precisely the beech, ash, aspen, and birch that dominate the hardwood scatter and sit outside Castle's coverage. Sample sizes are red maple 3,025, red oak 1,728, yellow birch 1,192, sugar maple 1,055, paper birch 655, quaking aspen 500, American beech 269, and white ash 129, so the extension has published coefficients for the exact species named, though white ash and beech are thin and beech is confounded by the roughly 70% high-risk rate driven by beech bark disease.

Predictors and model form. All sawlog presence and product ratio models are generalized linear with a logit link (beta regression with a logit link for the 0 to 1 product ratio), fit in a mixed-effects framework with stand and plot random effects, using VSURF plus AIC for variable selection. The hardwood sawlog presence model (Eq 13) uses log(DBH), log(HT/DBH), basal area of larger trees (BAL), the ideal-form indicator, the risk class, and species-by-DBH terms. The hardwood product ratio model (Eq 16) uses log(DBH), log(HT/DBH), form, risk, and species-by-DBH. Diameter enters nonlinearly: sawlog presence rises with DBH, while the sawlog proportion of merchantable volume rises then falls, peaking near 46 to 48 cm DBH and declining past about 60 cm. The field protocol measured trees over 10 cm DBH, defined a sawlog as at least one 2.43 m section with two clear faces and no disqualifying defect at a species-specific minimum sawlog DBH (25.4 cm for hardwoods), and computed volumes with regional Kozak taper and the International 1/4-inch board rule.

Key results and performance. Form and risk are significant predictors of sawlog presence and product ratio in every non-spruce-fir group (p < .001). The hardwood sawlog presence model has excellent discrimination (AUC 0.93 on fixed effects, 86% accuracy); spruce-fir is 0.90 and other softwoods 0.84. The hardwood product ratio model has low precision but low bias (RMSE 0.15, R2 0.29, bias -0.0068 on the 0 to 1 scale). The critical negative result: predicting form and risk themselves from stand and site variables was largely unsuccessful for hardwoods (AUC 0.63 to 0.66) and good only for the low-variability spruce-fir (up to 0.89). So form and risk carry strong signal for sawlog presence and value, but they are hard to synthesize from DBH, species, and site for hardwoods. That is the same wall the FVS-ACD benchmark faces, and it is the main risk for any prediction-fed modifier. Maximum sawlog proportions by species (Table A.10) run paper birch 71%, red maple 72%, red oak 73%, sugar maple 74%, yellow birch 75%, and white ash 83%, each near 46 to 48 cm DBH.

How it extends Castle 2017 and 2018. Broader species coverage (eight hardwoods plus softwoods versus four hardwoods), a far larger sample (18,003 versus Castle's), added predictors (BAL, HT/DBH, one-sided competition, climate), and an explicit product ratio model. Chapter 2 speaks straight to the FVS-ACD hardwood over-prediction: on ten managed northern Maine stands Smith compared five merchandizing methods (ASG, merchantable stopper heights, stick cruise, the form and risk approach in a two-stage and a probabilistic variant, and FVS). FVS default outputs gave much higher sawlog volume and stand value than any field method. FVS predicted 59% (Ashland) and 63% (Rangeley) of merchantable volume as sawlog, against 28 to 34% for the form and risk and ASG methods and 19 to 22% for stick cruising; FVS sawlog volume ran 44 to 66% above the field methods, and FVS standing value was significantly higher than all of them (p < .05). That is independent empirical confirmation that a growth model with no form and risk term systematically over-predicts hardwood sawlog volume and value, the same direction and roughly the same magnitude as the +19 to +20% bias and the value error the frozen benchmark shows.

Usable outputs. The thesis gives full parameter estimates with standard errors and fit statistics for every equation: Table 1.5 for the hardwood sawlog presence (Eq 13) and product ratio (Eq 16) models, and appendix Tables A.1 to A.8 for the form and risk prediction models and the spruce-fir and other-softwood presence and ratio models. The functional forms are explicit (logistic and beta-logit), so the models are implementable as written, and the volume support equations (Kozak taper, Smalian, International 1/4-inch) are in Appendix B. Two caveats on direct reuse. First, Smith's models are keyed to his consolidation (binary ideal form, three risk classes), which is not the Castle engine's consolidation (four form, two risk), so wiring them into the existing ddbh_hw_mod and tsurv_hw_mod structure needs either a class-system mapping or a light refit; the product ratio model, which the engine lacks entirely, can be adopted more directly as a new modifier. Second, the models take form and risk as inputs, and Smith shows those are hard to predict for hardwoods, so feeding them in the unobserved benchmark still means predicting form and risk first (poorly) and carrying that uncertainty forward. On data, the underlying field data (the 18,003-tree form and risk set and the 10-stand merchandized set with observed form and risk and measured sawlog heights) sits with Seven Islands Land Company, collected under NSF CAFS INTERN award 1915078. If sourced through Weiskittel and Seven Islands, that observed form and risk data is exactly what Phase 3 needs to validate FVS-ACD predicted form and risk on real stands rather than only its downstream volume and value effect.

## Staged plan

Phase 1, prototype the wiring (immediate, low risk, no new data). Wire the predicted form and risk into ddbh_hw_mod and tsurv_hw_mod using an expected value formulation: because prediction yields class probabilities while the modifier wants a discrete class, apply the probability weighted average of the class specific modifiers per tree. Turn on ops$use.hw.mod in a v13.x prototype branch. A and B test on the frozen MagPlot benchmark, modifier off (current baseline) versus on, scored on the hardwood stratified %RMSE for BA, TOTVOL, and the increment. Any change is cleanly attributable because everything else is frozen. Deliverable: does the growth and survival lever move hardwood %RMSE at all, and in the right direction.

Phase 2, the value lever (the largest error). Restore or rebuild the form and risk keyed sawlog and volume or product proportion modifier (the old hw.saw.prop) and connect it to value_fns.R so hardwood merchantable volume and value respond to predicted form and risk. Smith 2022 is the direct feed here: his hardwood sawlog presence model (Eq 13) and product ratio model (Eq 16), with full coefficients in his Table 1.5, give a per-tree expected sawlog fraction of merchantable volume as presence times proportion, keyed to species, DBH, HT/DBH, BAL, form, and risk. Since the v13.0 engine has no value form or risk term at all, this product ratio can be adopted as a new modifier fairly directly, subject to the class-system mismatch noted above (Smith's binary form and three risk classes versus the engine's four form and two risk), which may call for a mapping or a light refit against the engine's classes. Smith's Chapter 2 also sizes the prize: FVS with no form and risk term over-predicted hardwood sawlog volume by 44 to 66% and standing value significantly on real Maine stands, the same failure mode as the frozen benchmark. A and B test on hardwood VALUE and SAWVOL %RMSE, the metrics with the worst frozen scatter (value about 81 percent). This is the most likely place to actually reduce the hardwood frontier.

Phase 3, validate and extend. Secure the Castle 2017 and Smith 2022 observed form and risk data; Smith's sits with Seven Islands Land Company (NSF CAFS INTERN award 1915078) and can be sourced through Weiskittel, and it is the observed form and risk needed to validate FVS-ACD predicted form and risk directly on real stands rather than only its downstream volume and value effect. Extend the four species coverage (RO SM YB RM) toward the species that dominate the hardwood scatter but are unmodeled: American beech, ashes, aspens, and the other birches. Smith already provides published coefficients for exactly these, American beech, paper birch, quaking aspen, and white ash, in his hardwood sawlog presence and product ratio models (Table 1.5, species-by-DBH terms), so the extension has a concrete starting point rather than needing new fits, though white ash and beech are thin (n 129 and 269) and beech is confounded by beech bark disease. Note Smith's caution that form and risk are hard to predict for hardwoods (AUC 0.63 to 0.66), so the honest path is validation against his observed data. Consider enriching predictors beyond species and DBH with the observed proxies (crown ratio, cull, damage or decay) available in FIA and MagPlot, several of which (BAL, HT/DBH, one-sided competition, climate) Smith found significant.

Phase 4, integrate or park. If Phases 1 and 2 reduce hardwood %RMSE without harming the softwood and mixedwood cells or the Bakuzis realism, fold the form and risk modifier into a v13.x candidate behind a gate, run the full frozen benchmark and the biological realism checks, and gate against cb_v12101 the same way every v13 candidate is gated. If it does not help, park it with the negative result documented, which is itself useful.

## Success metric and risks

Success is a measurable reduction in hardwood %RMSE, primarily TOTVOL and VALUE, on the frozen benchmark A and B, with no regression in softwood or mixedwood and no new Bakuzis flag. Risks: the four species coverage is narrower than the hardwood scatter; predicted rather than observed form and risk adds a layer of uncertainty that could add noise rather than signal; the expected value formulation is an approximation of a discrete effect; and without observed form and risk data the prediction cannot be validated on these stands, only its downstream effect on volume and value. The honest fallback is that even a null result cleanly rules the lever out.

## First concrete step

Phase 1 is prototypable immediately on Cardinal against the frozen benchmark, using only code and coefficients that already exist. That is the recommended next action when v13 work resumes: wire calc_form_prob and calc_risk_prob into the existing modifiers by expected value, flip ops$use.hw.mod, and run the hardwood A and B.
