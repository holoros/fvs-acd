## Headline

Pass 3 localized the developing-plot volume bias to the diameter distribution and named ddbh() as the
suspect. Pass 4 tested that suspicion properly and the answer is sharper than the hypothesis. The
increment function is wrong, but not in the coefficient pass 3 suspected and not in the way the first
successful-looking fix implied. A joint tree-level refit falsifies both competing stories: the size
term b2 is not the lever (sign wrong, 11.4 SE from the tested value) and a pure level shift is not the
lever either (11 AIC out of 467). The correction lives in b1, the log-size elasticity, which the data
put at 0.498 against the current 0.377.

Two benchmark anchors were also repaired, and a third finding is that the benchmark corrections matter
more than any model change: PEF and Baxter each moved by more than any candidate moved them.

## The ddbh question, answered

ddbh() at line 1015 is separable. Competition, crown ratio and site enter as pure multiplicative
scalars and cannot move the peak, so the interior maximum is species specific and competition
independent. The base-only crossing is 14.6777 cm, confirming the pass 3 arithmetic. 61 of 63 species
carry an interior maximum, from GB at 4.1 cm to RO at 77.7 cm.

The interior maximum is real in the fitted function but does not appear as a turnover in the realized
surface, because BAL falls and crown ratio rises with tree size in real stands and those population
gradients more than offset b2 times dbh. Conditioning on BAL exposes it: in the high-BAL tercile the
model to observed increment ratio collapses from 0.94 to 0.56 across the size range, while in the
low-BAL tercile it is flat at 0.95 to 0.96.

A true tree chain was built from ME_TREE.csv with PREV_TRE_CN, giving 24,517 matched live survivors at
species agreement 0.979, which closes pass 3 open item 5 and unblocks tree-level increment validation.
Observed survivor 20.5 year increment by starting class is monotone increasing from 3.16 to 6.37 cm
with no interior maximum anywhere. The model runs 13 to 28 percent short per tree, deepening with
size, and the deficit in stems above 12.7 cm is 78 percent a failure to promote from below rather than
a failure to grow the stems already there.

## The refit, and two falsifications

Fitted on 24,507 survivors (attrition of 10 for non-finite values, 100 percent of usable) with the
model's own year-0 covariate state tapped directly from calc_ddbh, so no covariate was reimputed. The
tap is provably prediction neutral, byte identical to v131base_mefia_l_results.csv.

| fit | b0 | b1 | b2 | crossing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| current | -1.64234 | 0.37698 | -0.0256837 | 14.68 |
| F2 Gamma-log, survivors | -1.85116 | 0.51998 | -0.0253511 | 20.51 |
| F3 gaussian-log, all y | -1.88366 | 0.49788 | -0.0293426 | 16.97 |
| F4 selection corrected | -1.92953 | 0.51541 | -0.0260544 | 19.78 |

b2 is falsified. Delta b2 is -0.0037 (SE 0.0010) arithmetic, +0.0003 (SE 0.0016) Gamma. Stand-cluster
bootstrap over 300 reps gives a 95 percent interval of -0.00728 to +0.00007, with P(delta b2 at or
above 0.0034) = 0.000 and P(delta b2 at or above 0.0077) = 0.000. The 0.0077 the candidate applies is
11.4 SE away and the sign is wrong, not just the magnitude.

The level hypothesis is falsified too. Nested AIC on deviation models: b0+b1 = 0.00, b0+b1+b2 = 1.90,
b1 only = 23.11, b2 only = 79.41, b0+b2 = 81.39, b0 only = 455.81, null = 467.14. A pure level shift
buys 11 AIC out of 467 and b2 on top of b0+b1 contributes nothing.

The 0.0041 per cm shape estimate that motivated the gated candidate is reproducible as an artifact:
fitting a single b2 slope to the class table with the intercept forced to zero returns exactly
0.004099. It is a level absorbed into a slope, the same error the b2 candidate made one level up.

Identification caveat that must be carried: b0 and b1 correlate at -0.945 and are not separately
identified. Only their combination is. Quote the surface, never delta b0 or delta b1 alone. Centering
leaves delta b1 and delta b2 numerically unchanged, so the b2 verdict is identified by the data rather
than by parameterization. The fitted surface band is 1.9 to 9.5 percent wide across 2 to 60 cm, so the
surface is well determined where the coefficients are not.

Mean absolute bias on the seven scored non-harvest sources: base 7.884, b2 5.624, r1 5.129, r2 7.440.

The r1 against r2 split is not a tuning choice and must be understood before picking one. A Gamma-log
GLM matches E[y/mu], the mean of per-tree relative error. A gaussian-log GLM matches mean(y)/mean(mu),
the ratio of means. The volume benchmark scores a ratio of means, so r2 is the specification aligned
with the target and r1 is aligned with per-tree relative error. Whole sample the two differ a lot:
ratio of means 1.0563, mean ratio 1.1957. That is exactly why r1 buys 9 pp on Maine FIA and costs
7.3 pp on Baxter while r2 buys 1.1 and costs 1.2. Both agree that b2 is not the lever.

Weighting reverses the headline and this needs to be stated in any writeup. Unweighted observed over
model is 1.0563, but expansion-factor weighted it is 0.9709. On a stems per hectare basis the model
already grows 2.9 percent too fast, and the 2.54 to 5 cm class is overpredicted by 11.9 percent. The
deficit lives entirely in the mid to large classes that carry volume.

A physical plausibility screen, PPS-1, was adopted after an impossible stem surfaced. A t2 record is
rejected only if all three hold: implied PAI_D above 2.5 cm per year, diameter above 3 times the
largest other stem of the same species on the plot-occasion, and diameter above 60 cm. It removes 2
Baxter records, both exact decimal shifts (190.50 cm from 17.27, and 91.44 cm from 5.33), and
diagnostically removes 2 in mefia, 1 in ns and 1 in qcnb carrying 0.004 to 0.027 percent of basal area
against 1.80 percent in Baxter. Adopting it globally does not reopen any other scorecard. The screen
rejects a measurement rather than a tree, so the matched t1 record is retained, which penalizes rather
than flatters the model.

Baxter final: regenerated base +8.77. The +2.46, +6.89 and +7.50 figures in circulation are all
superseded. +7.50 was stale-prediction, computed before the init rebuild was carried through.

## Normalizers, reopened and not closed

The mass-weighted derivation is correct and the shipped unweighted normalizer is the wrong statistic.
The invariant is set by what each hook multiplies, so it is algebra, not a choice. Ingrowth must be
weighted by the engine's base recruitment (prob_ingrowth times the density release), not by observed
recruits; the recruit-weighted 5.46 is an artifact that double counts the modifier, matching the
identity E[e^2eta]/E[e^eta] at 5.4072. Mortality must be weighted by base expected self-thinning
deaths, ramp(rd) times stems per hectare. That matters enormously because ramp is exactly zero below
RD 0.30 and has a 40-fold slope break at 0.62, so 8.25 percent of fitting rows carry 91.6 percent of
the weight. Deaths-weighting and trials-weighting are both wrong.

| hook | unweighted | mass-weighted | direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| ingrowth | 1.9202005115 | 2.1753091221 | recruitment down a further 11.7 percent |
| mortality | 1.0119860967 | 0.8469918858 | self-thinning mortality up 19.5 percent |

The correct formula applied to the wrong population. The mass-weighted values FAIL aggregate
neutrality on PEF and fail worse than the unweighted ones: mortality moves from +0.28 percent off to
-2.91 percent off. The cause is that the posteriors were fitted to FIA CONUS remeasurement pairs while
the engine's recruitment and self-thinning were calibrated on Acadian data. Each benchmark's own
correct mortality normalizer is 0.9734 to 1.1676, all 15 to 38 percent away from the training-set
0.8470. Recomputed over the Acadian benchmark pool the same derivation gives 0.9749 and 2.3195.

Scorecard consequence: the mass-weighted candidate is worse than the current combined candidate on
every source measured, by 0.18 to 1.79 pp. That is not a reason to reject the derivation; it is
evidence that the global-normalizer premise, not the weighting, is what needs revisiting. A single
global scalar cannot fix this: it divides every source by the same number, so the 19-fold between-set
spread in realized aggregate modifier is invariant to the choice of N. For ingrowth the within-training
stratum spread is 65-fold because the modifier essentially is a QMD elasticity of -2.68, and no scalar
can make that neutral on a set whose QMD distribution differs from the reference.

## The cap, ratified and working

Threshold 4.81 cm (bootstrap median over 300 reps, 95 percent interval 4.73 to 4.91), set where the
fitted elasticity stops reproducing the observed one rather than at a round number, and above the hard
support limit of 3.086 cm. Placed inside .acd_hier_eta_dev upstream of the normalizer as required.
Binds on 0.90 percent of ingrowth fitting rows. NB maximum ingrowth falls 3126.9 to 246.7 and the
fraction above 200 trees per hectare per year falls 16.10 to 6.14 percent. The cap, not the normalizer,
is what narrowed the between-set spread, from 36.2-fold to about 19-fold.

Unadvertised side effect found: the cap binds on the MORTALITY hook too, up to 11.9 percent of
plot-years on NB. Since the mortality b_lnqmd is positive, capping QMD upward raises the mortality
modifier on exactly those plots. This was never part of the ratified rationale and should be stated.
