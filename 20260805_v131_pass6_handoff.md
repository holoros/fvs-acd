## Headline

Pass 5 closed with a 9.2 pp "demographic residual" and pointed the next pass at the self-thinning
drain and the ingrowth level. Pass 6 attacked both and found that the residual as stated rested on two
bad numbers, one of which was mine. Corrected, the picture inverts: the model is not over-draining by
a factor of 2.4 and is not over-recruiting at all. It is UNDER-recruiting by a factor of 2.5 to 3.9
and short on gross accretion by 11 percent, and the deficit is about two thirds accretion and one
third drain rather than 60 percent drain.

Both errors were caught independently by two tracks that did not communicate, which is the strongest
form of confirmation available here. Both are corrected below with the arithmetic shown.

## Two numbers withdrawn

**The 0.377 ingrowth figure was a units artifact and it was mine.** ING_p in the results files is
stems per hectare, not basal area. Verified directly: mean ING_p on mefia_l is 7.7191 stems ha-1 and
sum(ING_p)/sum(iv) is 0.3769, so 0.377 is a stems-per-year rate that I labelled as m2 ha-1 yr-1 in the
pass 5 handoff and then briefed into two pass 6 tracks. True model ingrowth basal area at entry is
7.719 times pi times 0.05^2 over 4, which is **0.015 m2 ha-1 yr-1**, a factor of 25 smaller. Every
statement in the pass 5 handoff about excess ingrowth is void.

**The drain decomposition was backwards.** Pass 5 reported total drain 0.740 with calc_mortality at
0.302 (41 percent) and the SDImax block at 0.438 (59 percent), derived by residual attribution.
Direct instrumentation of AcadianGYOneStand, logging basal area removed per stand-year by each
component separately, gives on mefia_l over 18,801 stand-years:

| component | m2 ha-1 yr-1 | share |
|---|---|---|
| calc_mortality | 0.3182 | 73.2 |
| SDImax block | 0.1166 | 26.8 |
| total drain | 0.4348 | |

The measurement is provably complete: scored BA_p equals the last instrumented post-block basal area
to a mean difference of -0.0006 m2 ha-1 (r = 0.99989), and the balance closes exactly at gross
accretion 0.7899 = net 0.3551 + drain 0.4348. The prior figure assigned everything calc_mortality did
not explain to the SDImax block, and the SDImax block is in fact the smaller half.

## The corrected accounting

Observed drain was also undervalued because the chain values dead and removed stems at their t1
diameter. 3,675 of 5,999 dead stems carry a measured t2 diameter and the basal-area-weighted realised
growth fraction of the interval is 0.275. Recalibrated: observed mortality **0.1862**, observed
mortality plus removals **0.3869** m2 ha-1 yr-1. Against a model drain of 0.4348 that is a **12
percent excess, not 140 or 390 percent**.

Of the 2.98 m2 ha-1 Maine FIA basal area deficit, roughly 1.0 m2 ha-1 is excess drain and 2.0 m2 ha-1
is short gross accretion (model 0.790 against observed 0.888, 11 percent low). Harvest selection does
not bias this: unharvested-only observed mortality is 0.1575 against 0.1524 all-stand.

## The ingrowth double count, a real if small fix

prob_ingrowth predicts the total flow across min.dbh.ingrowth, but the FIA tree list already carries
1,934 TPH below 5.0 cm that crosses natively, so the flow is counted twice. Netting it
(ingrowth_net = max(0, gross - alpha*cross), with cross exact from ddbh and dexpf at line 4053) gives
gate-off byte identity at 0.000e+00 on the gateoff and self tests, and differs only on the young stand,
which is the structural claim. Worth +0.345 pp alone and +0.265 pp on top of r2 on mefia_l, and it
costs Baxter only 0.076 pp against the flat IMULT knob's 0.50 pp. The selective repair GAINS 0.345 pp
where blunt removal of the same recruitment COSTS 0.562 pp, so ops$ingrowth.mult is not a substitute.

## Drain and ingrowth interact only weakly, but the stem budget is fragile

Two-by-two factorial on mefia_l: base -16.531, ingrowth off -17.093, drain off -9.882, both off
-10.215. Interaction +0.229 pp, so near additive. **The critical number is the stem budget.** Stem
bias is +1.45 percent at base, **+6.43 percent if the drain is fixed alone**, and +0.35 percent with
both. Total stem count is currently held roughly unbiased by two compensating errors, and a drain fix
shipped alone will break it. Halving ingrowth costs only 0.15 to 0.18 pp of volume, so ingrowth cannot
be used to offset a drain fix.

## The pooled refit, corrected: cannot separate, r2 stands

The pass 5 pooled artifact is fully explained and the repair is done. The truncation was in the fit
script (y > 0), not the assembly. Non-positive share by source: ctrn 30.6 percent (25.6 percent
exactly zero, median dd 0.250 cm = the 0.1 inch quantum), nb 12.8, silc_l 12.9, ns_unmanaged 10.0,
baxter 8.7, ns_managed 6.6, pef 4.7, mefia_l 1.2. **Retention alone moves CTRN's required multiplier
from 1.766 to 1.192**, so truncation rather than interval length was the larger half of the artifact
and the interval screen is second order.

A gaussian log link constrains mu > 0 but places no constraint on y, so retaining non-positives is
exact for the r2 analogue with no shift. The glmer trap is that gaussian()$initialize sets mustart = y
and fails; mustart = pmax(y, 1e-3) fixes it.

Verdict on r2: **cannot separate, r2 stands ratified.** Three independent lines converge. The
source-level bootstrap band covers r2 at 59 of 59 diameters. The LR test demands source random slopes
on the size terms (Chisq 9707, 5 df, p < 2.2e-16), and the properly specified model gives b1 = 0.454
+/- 0.103 and b2 = -0.0392 +/- 0.0134, containing r2's 0.498 and -0.0293 at 0.43 and 0.74 SE, so the
correctly specified pooled model is CLOSER to r2 than the naive one. Maine FIA alone under this
pipeline reproduces r2 to three or four decimals, confirming the pipeline.

**r2 does not carry truncation bias and r1 does.** r2 is F3 at p4e/p4e_fit.R line 155, fitted on all y
including y <= 0; the P <- L2[y > 0] subset at line 140 feeds only F1, F2, F4 and F5. That is a
substantive reason to prefer r2 beyond the ratio-of-means argument, and it means every Gamma-log or
log-scale increment fit in this project carries a 1 to 7 percent upward truncation bias while
gaussian-log fits on the full set do not.

## A second CTRN-class artifact found

The naive corrected pooled fit's large small-diameter boost (1.38 at 2 cm) comes entirely from one
source. Per-source required multipliers in the 0 to 6 and 6 to 8 cm bands are 2.323 and 1.853 for
ns_managed against about 0.98 for everyone else. Those 5,250 records have D1 down to 0.30 cm and
expansion factors up to 836 ha-1 across 22 distinct levels against a median of 25 for its records at
or above 8 cm. It is a nested regeneration subplot with a different design, the exact structural
analogue of the CTRN artifact. Dropping those 5,250 rows, 2.1 percent of the set, moves b1 from 0.163
to 0.409 and the crossing from 9.05 to 14.31 cm. **Exclude ns_managed sub-8 cm regeneration-subplot
records from any future pooled fit.**

Chain quality flagged: silc_l is bad (7.7 percent negative increments, minimum -26.2 cm, 0.1
percentile -19.9 cm); nb and baxter are suspect. mefia_l, pef and the NS sources are clean. Trimming
below -2 cm changes nothing, so the negatives are not driving results, but silc_l needs a look.

NEW: filtering a fitting set to positive increments biases short-interval sources upward severely.
Retain non-positive increments; a gaussian log link handles them exactly.

NEW: a source-intercept-only random effect on a pooled fit overstates size-term precision by about an
order of magnitude. Source random SLOPES on the size terms are required (Chisq 9707, p < 2.2e-16).
