## 1. Scope and method

This is the long-term companion to the guardrail Bakuzis check already on record (`20260721_v1211_guardrail_results.md`, guardrail 2), which scored a single softwood synthetic stand over a 160-yr, ESI 12/16/20 gradient and found the v12.11 candidate's primary (unmanaged) mapping passed all five relations. That check answers whether v12.11 broke anything on one stand type; this one asks whether biological realism holds across the productivity gradient **and** across the species composition gradient (softwood, mixedwood, hardwood) that the Acadian region actually contains, and adds OSM-ACD v2.26.1 as a third point of comparison using its own existing long-horizon run.

The `bakuzis-assessment` skill supplied the modified Bakuzis matrix framework (Leary 1997) and the five objective PASS/FLAG tests used throughout: Eichhorn (volume should be a near-unique function of mean height across sites, tested as the coefficient of variation of volume at matched height bins, PASS if CV<15%), Reineke self-thinning (slope of log(TPH) on log(QMD) in the mature, density-declining phase, PASS if in -1.8 to -1.4, centered on the theoretical -1.605), site ordering (better sites must not fall below poorer sites in height or volume at any age, PASS if zero inversions), yield-density plateau (basal area must stabilize in a realistic range rather than run away or collapse, PASS if BAmax<=42 m² ha⁻¹ and the last-20-yr standard deviation is small), and Sukachev (mean tree size must rise as density falls in the mature phase, PASS if the QMD-on-TPH slope is negative). These are the same objective tests used in the existing guardrail and in `bakuzis_allmodels.R`; nothing about the test logic was changed here.

**Models and configuration.** BASE is the actual frozen `~/_scripts/AcadianGY_12.10.1.r` file (not the "v12.11 with levers off" proxy the guardrail used, though that proxy is independently confirmed byte-identical elsewhere). CANDIDATE is `~/_scripts/AcadianGY_12.11.r` with the locked lever block (`use.ddbh.standcon`, `use.dht.hw.mod`, `mort.dens.scale`, `ingrowth.mult=2.5`) in its unmanaged mapping, `V1211_MANAGED=FALSE`, the configuration the guardrail found passes 5/5 and the correct mapping for an un-thinned synthetic gradient. OSM-ACD v2.26.1 is scored from its own existing 159-yr run (see below), reused unchanged.

**Stand starts.** Three seeded 40-tree stands (3000 stems ha⁻¹ initial, 2-5" DBH, identical structure and RNG seed across models so only the model and species mix differ): softwood (SW: balsam fir, red spruce, black spruce, white spruce), mixedwood (MW: balsam fir, red spruce, red maple, sugar maple), hardwood (HW: red maple, sugar maple, yellow birch, paper birch), each projected across an ESI(csi) 12/16/20 low/mid/high productivity gradient, region ME, for 160 years. The hardwood form/risk record split (`hw_formrisk_split.R`, Smith Table A.9, unedited) is applied for the CANDIDATE runs, matching the guardrail's own methodology.

## 3. Per-relation evidence

**Eichhorn's rule.** Both FVS configurations pass cleanly on all three stand types (CV of volume at matched height bins: BASE 11.0-14.2%, CANDIDATE 11.0-14.1%, both well under the 15% threshold; hardwood runs slightly higher than softwood or mixedwood in both models, 14.1-14.2%, but still passes). OSM flags this relation (CV 26.3%), meaning OSM's volume-height relationship is noticeably more site-dependent than either FVS configuration; volume production is not as close to a unique function of mean height across OSM's LOW/MID/HIGH real-stand terciles as it is in the synthetic FVS gradient.

**Reineke self-thinning.** All three models pass, and the numbers move together in an interesting way: CANDIDATE's slope is consistently slightly less steep than BASE's on the same stand type (SW -1.62 vs -1.59, HW -1.49 vs -1.50) except mixedwood where it is close (-1.56 vs -1.54), all comfortably inside the -1.8 to -1.4 acceptance band and near the theoretical -1.605. OSM's pooled real-stand slope (-1.56) sits in the same range. Self-thinning behavior is the one relation where all three models agree most closely, and it is also the strongest of the five relations per Leary (1997), so this is a meaningfully reassuring result for both BASE and CANDIDATE at century scale.

**Site ordering.** This is the one relation with a genuine finding worth flagging, and it appears in **both** BASE and CANDIDATE, identically in character and magnitude, only on the mixedwood stand start: at ages 46-52 (BASE) or 46-49 (CANDIDATE), the mid-productivity site (ESI 16) height and volume trajectories edge fractionally above the high-productivity site (ESI 20) for a handful of years (e.g., BASE age 49: HT_16=12.84 m vs HT_20=12.60 m, a 1.9% crossing; VOL_16=138.7 vs VOL_20=137.8 m³ ha⁻¹, a 0.6% crossing), then the ordering re-establishes itself and holds for the remaining ~110 years of the 160-yr run. Softwood and hardwood both pass with zero inversions in both models. Because the crossing appears at the same ages, on the same stand type, with near-identical magnitude in BASE and CANDIDATE, it is best read as a pre-existing behavior of the shared underlying growth engine on this specific 50/50 softwood-hardwood species mix (likely a transient mortality- or ingrowth-timing asynchrony between site classes around stand-establishment age, not a structural site-effect problem in the height or volume equations), not something v12.11's levers introduced or worsened. OSM's site-ordering flag is a different order of severity: 42 height inversions and 105 volume inversions over the run, and at the 159-yr endpoint the poorest (LOW) site tercile ends with more standing basal area, SDI, and QMD than either MID or HIGH (BA_end: LOW 41.9, MID 34.7, HIGH 33.1 m² ha⁻¹; QMD_end: LOW 24.2, MID 20.3, HIGH 19.3 cm), a persistent, endpoint-level inversion rather than a brief mid-rotation crossing. Some of this may reflect real disturbance or measurement history in the underlying SILC/CFI stands rather than a pure OSM model artifact, since those are real remeasured stands and not a controlled synthetic gradient (a caveat noted above); the objective test does not distinguish the two causes, so OSM is scored FLAG here as the test specifies, with that caveat stated rather than suppressed.

**Yield-density / plateau.** All three models pass. BASE and CANDIDATE both land in a realistic 32-36 m² ha⁻¹ range at year 160 across all three stand types, well under the 42 m² ha⁻¹ runaway threshold, and CANDIDATE consistently plateaus 0.4-0.8 m² ha⁻¹ lower than BASE on the same stand type (a small, consistent effect of the density-dependent mortality gate absorbing the extra ingrowth from IGMULT=2.5, the same mechanism already documented in the guardrail's own 160-yr plateau check). OSM's mid-tercile trajectory plateaus at 34.7 m² ha⁻¹ (down slightly from a 35.8 peak), also inside the realistic range. All three trajectories share a visible early-rotation feature worth noting even though it does not fail any of the five named tests: basal area rises quickly to roughly 25-30 m² ha⁻¹ by age 25-30, dips by 15-20% over the next 15-20 years (a self-thinning pulse as the dense 3000-stem initial planting crashes down), then climbs steadily to the century-scale plateau. This shape is consistent across BASE and CANDIDATE and across all three stand types (see figure 24, panel A), so it is an artifact of the seeded initial density and the self-thinning mortality response rather than a model-specific quirk.

**Sukachev.** All three models pass on every stand type: quadratic mean diameter rises as density falls through the mature phase in every case (negative QMD-on-TPH slope), consistent with the Reineke result above, since the two relations are drawing on the same self-thinning trajectory.

## 5. Honest read and verdict

**Does v12.11 hold biological realism over the long term?** Yes, on the same terms the guardrail already established, now confirmed across the species-composition gradient rather than one softwood stand alone. CANDIDATE's headline PASS/FLAG pattern is identical to BASE's on all five relations at every stand type: where BASE passes, CANDIDATE passes; the one FLAG (site ordering, mixedwood only) is present in BASE too, at nearly the same ages and magnitude, so it is not a candidate-introduced regression. The two configurations differ numerically in the expected, and defensible, direction given the lever design: CANDIDATE's Reineke slope is very slightly shallower (its density-dependent mortality gate and higher ingrowth multiplier absorb recruitment slightly more gently), and its long-horizon plateau lands 0.4-0.8 m² ha⁻¹ lower on every stand type (denser, smaller-tree stands, consistent with the higher stem count at a similar basal-area ceiling already documented in the guardrail's plateau check). Neither difference is large enough to move any relation's verdict.

**How does this compare to OSM?** On this specific long-horizon biological-consistency test, both FVS configurations score better than OSM-ACD v2.26.1: FVS passes 4 of 5 relations cleanly (with the mixedwood site-ordering flag being minor and transient) while OSM fails 2 of 5 (Eichhorn and site ordering), and OSM's site-ordering failure is persistent and endpoint-level rather than a brief mid-rotation crossing. This should be read alongside, not instead of, the master accuracy scorecard (`20260723_master_scorecard_and_ranking.md`), where OSM wins or ties FVS on several accuracy metrics, especially on unmanaged stands; the Bakuzis test is a falsification check on internal biological consistency, not a measure of predictive accuracy, and a model can be more externally accurate on held-out stands while being less internally self-consistent across a productivity gradient, or vice versa. The honest summary is that v12.11 does not trade away long-term biological realism for its accuracy gains over BASE, and that OSM's real-stand-based long-horizon run surfaces a genuine site-ordering concern that the FVS family, in this synthetic-gradient test, does not share.

**Caveats.** The FVS runs are idealized seeded stands with a single RNG seed per model x standtype x site cell (no replication, so no uncertainty band around any PASS/FLAG boundary); a different seed or initial stem count could plausibly shift the mixedwood crossing's exact age or whether it triggers at all. The OSM comparison is not perfectly apples-to-apples: it scores real, previously disturbed SILC/CFI stands binned by site tercile rather than a controlled synthetic gradient, so part of its site-ordering and Eichhorn flags could reflect genuine stand history rather than a pure model artifact; this was stated above rather than adjusted away, since the task instructed not to fabricate and to reuse the existing frozen run as is. Nothing here was rerun or reinterpreted to make any model look better or worse than its numbers show.
