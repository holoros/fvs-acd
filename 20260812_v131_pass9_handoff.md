## Track B. The volume emulator has no Jensen exposure

The 12 August decision was that convexity ships in the volume emulator, `nsvb()` in `bench_p6score.R`.
Implementing it showed the premise does not hold there. `nsvb()` is a fully vectorised per-tree
allometric, and standing volume is assembled as a per-tree evaluation multiplied by the per-tree
expansion factor and summed. The allometric is never evaluated at a mean diameter or height, and missing
heights are filled per tree by a deterministic asymptote rather than a stand mean.

This was established by reconstruction rather than by reading code. A rebuild of the volume path from
the tree list, the coefficient file and the per-tree sum alone matches the harness-written total volume
to a maximum absolute difference of 1.02e-12 m³ ha⁻¹ across all 918 Maine FIA stands and 211,135 tree
records. Since the reconstruction uses nothing but per-tree diameter, height and expansion factor, the
exact match proves nothing else enters the quantity. The finding generalises: 42 files in `verify/`
define `nsvb` and 38 carry the byte-identical sum line.

The counterfactual is worth keeping. Evaluating at the abundance-weighted mean within stand and species
gives 131.10 m³ ha⁻¹ against the correct treewise 139.35, a 5.92 percent underestimate. The harness
already captures all of it, so shipping a correction there would have inflated every source by roughly 6
pp of fabrication. Nothing was implemented, which is the right outcome.

One method note carries forward. A second-order Taylor correction applied to that counterfactual
overshoots, returning 144.75 against the true 139.35, landing 3.88 percent high, because the weighted
within-group coefficient of variation of D²H is 0.686, far outside the range where a two-term expansion
is trustworthy on a right-skewed variable.

## Track F. The 3.87 pp convexity entry re-derived: real, but misnamed and located elsewhere

Aaron's call was to re-derive the budget line before anything else proceeded. It reproduces. Recomputing
from current files gives +3.867 pp against the standing +3.866, the 0.001 being a hardcoded -16.53
against the file's -16.531. Both files are 919 lines, observed columns byte identical.

The provenance chain is unbroken: `p6sdi/NOTES.md` line 5 back to `p5cvx/p5cvx_ceiling.csv` row 1,
written 5 August 2026 by `p5cvx/c4_adversarial.R` under job `p5cvx_syn`, value produced by

reproduces base at 0.000e+00, so the whole move is the inserted block.

What it measures is not convexity in the volume emulator. It is the growth-side term: the model predicts
a conditional mean diameter per tree, NSVB volume has relative curvature 4.2 to 6.4 in diameter, and
summing a convex function of conditional means understates the aggregate. The p5cvx correction acts
after `metrics()` returns and leaves the emulator, its coefficients and its per-tree summation untouched.
So Track B's finding and this entry never collided in substance; the collision was a naming artifact.

The apparent factor-of-seven conflict between the two tracks resolves exactly, and the resolution is a
space mismatch rather than an error in either. Track B wrote its sensitivity table in X = D²H holding
E[X] fixed, using the emulator's nearly linear exponent of 1.1206. The p5cvx term puts the spread on
diameter holding E[D] fixed, and because X is itself convex in D with effective exponent near 2.6, the
smear raises E[X] by 5.457 percent as well as spreading it. Measured on the calibration population, the
diameter-space smear gives +6.347 percent on aggregate volume against +0.864 percent for Track B's form
at the actual volume-weighted CV of X of 0.358, a factor of 7.35. The implied diameter CV is 0.124
volume-weighted, entirely credible over a 20 year interval on a tree list whose expansion-weighted mean
t2 diameter is 11.3 cm.

**Budget verdict: no arithmetic change.** Keep 3.866, keep the residual at 9.22 pp, do not reopen it to
13.09. Rename the entry from convexity to second-moment dispersion of predicted tree diameter, and carry
the caveat that the 15.44 pp ceiling repairs first and second moments together, so this piece is a
subset rather than an independent addition, which makes 9.22 an upper bound.

Two items stay open. 54.9 percent of the term rests on the assumption that height co-moves along
`himp()` with the smeared diameter; freezing height drops the calibration-population effect from 6.347

## Track C. The site gradient is inverted, not inert, and the equation is rank deficient

Pass 8 concluded that two opposite-signed site terms in `plot_ba_incr()` nearly cancel, net -0.016, so
the site gradient is inert. The algebra is right and the conclusion drawn from it is wrong.

The shared logarithm collapses exactly, confirmed to within 7.24e-07 across a csi 10 to 22 by rd 0.2 to
1.4 grid, so the net coefficients are -0.03265 on log(rd) and -0.01602 on log(csi). But the quantity
that matters is percent change in the increment, which is (q2+q4)/(csi·rt), and rt itself collapses
toward zero as relative density rises. Realized site sensitivity is therefore neither near zero nor
uniform: at 25 m² ha⁻¹ and csi 14 it runs -1.90 percent per unit csi at rd 0.2, -4.68 at rd 0.6, -7.59
at rd 0.8 and -14.69 at rd 1.0. Across the 933 benchmark plots the median is -3.24 percent per unit csi
and -21.9 percent over csi 12 to 20. **Higher site index produces less basal area growth, and the
inversion strengthens with density.** Beyond rd of about 1.07 to 1.50 depending on csi the 0.00227·BA
floor takes over and site sensitivity becomes exactly zero; that floor binds on 2.47 percent of
benchmark plots now and will bind far more in long projections.

The near cancellation is a rank deficiency built into the model form, not ecology. Because log(csi·rd)
is identically log(csi) plus log(rd), the four regressors span three dimensions and only the 1e-6
offsets keep the design invertible. Variance inflation factors are 1.28e10, 1.29e10 and 4.72e8 with a
condition number of 232,427, and refitting the as-fitted basis to observed plot basal area change
returns +6329.52, +6329.48 and -6329.55, each with a standard error around 2750. The shipped
coefficients are an arbitrary point on a one-dimensional ridge. More data cannot fix this;
reparameterization can. Do not quote the 0.108 pairwise correlation between log(csi) and log(csi·rd) as
evidence of no problem: the variance of log(rd) is 27 times that of log(csi), and the carrying
correlation is 0.982 between log(rd) and log(csi·rd).

Recommended path: land the collapsed basis first as a no-op refactor, rt = a0 + a1·log(rd) + a2·log(csi)
+ a3·log(hw), with a1 and a2 at current net values so predictions reproduce to 7.2e-07 and VIFs fall to
about 1.05, then gate the refit of a2 as a separate change. At the median plot the required a2 is
+0.0047 for a 7 percent gradient matching the height equation, +0.0101 for 15 percent and +0.0202 for 30
percent, against a current -0.016, so the needed shift is about 0.021.

Two cautions shape the scope. A second site inversion nobody has named runs through SDImax, which
carries 222.7836/csi, so higher csi lowers SDImax, raises relative density and lowers the increment
again through a1; refitting a2 alone leaves that pathway in place. And indicative fits show the sign of
the site effect flips with the response definition, +0.0348 for subplot-only survivor growth against
-0.118 for fixed-expansion-factor survivor growth, which swamps the identification issue and must be
settled first. Two data traps were found: FIA expansion factors change by a factor of 12.46 at the 12.7
cm microplot threshold so survivor increment must use a fixed expansion factor, and 31.6 percent of
matched trees shrink in diameter and need a screen.

Validation must reverse the Bakuzis site ordering relation as primary criterion, hold Eichhorn, which is
most at risk since a stronger a2 shifts basal area without touching height, and score a three arm
comparison including a2 forced to zero to test whether the current inversion quietly offsets another
bias. Per standing rule a2 reports with a cluster bootstrap interval resampled on STAND, at least 500
replicates, carried onto the derived percent change from csi 12 to 20, with empirical coverage checked.
Compute cost is trivial; the real cost is assembling the pooled NS, NB, QC, CTRN and Baxter benchmarks,
which the narrow Maine csi range makes necessary.

## Track D. The r2 budget discrepancy is reconciled and both numbers are right

The 1.13 pp is total volume and the 0.195 pp is basal area, from the same pair of runs, the same 918
stands, the same observed values and the same formula, with no baseline mismatch and no ceiling-relative
normalization. From `v131base_mefia_l_results.csv` and `p6ing/p7r2only_mefia_l_results.csv`, both 919
lines with identical 38 column schemas: total volume -16.5312 against -15.4010, difference 1.1301, and
basal area -9.8239 against -9.6290, difference 0.1949. Both reproduce to three decimals. STAND vectors
are identical after sorting and observed columns byte identical. The alternatives are dead: the ratio
5.80 is neither the ceiling nor its reciprocal, and identical row counts and observed values rule out a
subset or a different baseline generation.

The budget is a volume budget and 1.130 pp stays. The entry needs a units label, not a new value.
Replacement language: increment reshaping under r2 removes 1.130 pp of the 15.44 pp total-volume bias
ceiling on mefia_l (total volume level bias -16.531 to -15.401, n = 918); the corresponding basal area
gain is 0.195 pp (basal area level bias -9.824 to -9.629). The 0.195 must never be substituted into the
volume ledger.

The pass 6 tension between r2 slowing sub-8 cm trees and excess flow across the 5.0 cm threshold is not
real. The r2 to base multiplier is analytic: 0.848 at 2 cm, 0.937 at 5 cm, 0.981 at 8 cm, crossing 1.0

excess-flow finding demands, so it is a partial fix rather than a contradiction. Magnitudes do not line
up, since a 6 percent cut at 5 cm cannot close a 2.3-fold excess, so the small-tree defect stays open.

## Track E. The pattern sweep is clean, the silent-failure sweep is not

The self-referential default argument sweep came back clean. A parse-tree walk covering 107 top-level
expressions, 135 function nodes, 524 formals and 143 non-empty defaults, reading formals pairlists
directly and building a dependency graph on each closure's own formals so transitive cycles are caught,
returned exactly three hits, all already known: `taper_kozak` at 4340 and `taper_westfall_scott` at 4386,
both live and reached from production through `calc_taper()`, and `calc_dib` at 4479, dead with no call
site anywhere. All three are already fixed by the pass 8 overlay. To show the negative is real rather
than a coverage gap, the identical sweeper was run against a control file with seven planted defects
across six structural shapes, including a three-cycle, a definition nested inside another function, an
anonymous closure inside `pmap` and a closure in a list literal; it caught all seven and correctly
cleared both benign look-alikes.

The silent-failure sweep found a live defect worth 97 percent. At model line 2999 the ingrowth
aggregate-neutrality normalizer falls back to `norm = 1` when its file is absent, and line 89 loads that
file as optional while the two required posterior files raise a hard stop. The substituted value is a
valid positive scalar, so the guard passes with no error, warning or message. The model resolves the
prefix to `v131r_ingrowth` and wants `v131r_ingrowth_norm.csv`, which does not exist in `~/v13_kickoff`,
the postdir hardcoded by seven sbatch scripts and the default in both `bench_p6.R` and
`bench_parity_split_ms_v131.R`. The only normalizer carrying that data is `v131r_ingrowth3_norm.csv`,
for a different refit generation. Two recorded runs executed the hierarchical branch against that
directory.

Reproduced with two postdirs differing only by the file's presence: all ten fixef and scaling scalars
identical, eta_dev maximum absolute difference 0.000e+00, mean modifier 1.187936 with the file against
2.340564 without, ratio 1.970278. On the calibrated base that is 13.39 becoming 31.34 instead of 15.91
stems ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹, against observed MagPlot rates of roughly 39.8 for NB and 19.7 for QC. The fix is
`p9sweep/hier_norm_guard_patch.R`, an overlay that saves the original closure and calls it verbatim,
turning a missing normalizer into a hard stop keyed on file absence rather than on `norm == 1` so a
genuine unit normalizer still loads. Verified by numeric identity rather than absence of an error:
`identical()` true on the whole returned list, maximum absolute difference 0 across all eleven scalars
and across 5000 random covariate draws.

The blast radius is bounded and does not reach this pass. Every run with `use.hier.ingrowth` false,
which is all 80 benchmark runs including every p6ing cell and every pass 9 cell, uses
`ops$ingrowth.mult` and never touches the normalizer. Any result with the switch on and pointed at
`~/v13_kickoff` needs a rerun against `~/v13_ingrowth3n_post` or `~/v13_comb_post`.

Two items rank below it. `calc_merch_height` at line 4522 still wraps `optimize` in `try(silent = TRUE)`
and is still unpatched, because pass 8's overlay redefined the taper functions and `calc_tree_volume`
but never `calc_merch_height`; it is latent now that the trigger is fixed, and converting it to a stop
is an edge-tree policy call rather than a defect fix. And roughly 45 instances of the ops-reader idiom
apply `&&` to a possibly length-greater-than-one operand, which under R 4.3 and later is an error, so a
vector-valued option is silently discarded. Demonstrated on `ops$sdimort.onset`, the exact parameter
Track A sweeps: `c(0.55, 0.55)` silently yields 0.30 and the character `"0.55"` passes through unchecked.

## Standing warnings, carried forward plus new this pass

BASE_PREFIX is always v131base. Verify by CSV row count, and also by realized magnitude against intended
target, and where a gate-off path exists also by numeric identity. Do not use QMD as evidence. The
Chapman-Richards height correction applies to PEF and regenerated Baxter only. Do not enable lever 3a.
Do not attribute a drain component by residual. Do not value dead stems at t1 diameter, use realised
growth fraction 0.275. Do not filter a fitting set to positive increments. Do not apply canonical
Bakuzis criteria to raw annual trajectories. A job or task status carried in a handoff is subject to F9
and must be re-queried live. A `function(x = x)` default and a `try(silent = TRUE)` around a solver are
both live bug patterns in this codebase. Do not pursue density-dependent recruitment as a fix for the
ing39 crash.

NEW: a client-side MCP timeout on `cardinal_submit_job` does NOT mean the submission failed. The first
Track A submission returned `MCP error -32001` and had in fact succeeded remotely; resubmitting produced
a duplicate array (13434134 alongside 13434152) writing to identical CONFIG_TAG paths, a live collision
risk on shared result files. Always re-query `squeue` by job name before resubmitting anything that
timed out, and prefer `sbatch` through `cardinal_run` over the submit tool for this reason.

NEW: the benchmark plot key is STAND. PLOT is a within-stand index and grouping on it silently collapses
every source to a single plot.

NEW: a benchmark's INPUT files can be rebuilt after a baseline result file is generated, which makes the
baseline stale without changing its row count. Compare input modification times against baseline result
modification times before trusting any level comparison. This is now a proven, not hypothetical, defect
on Baxter.
