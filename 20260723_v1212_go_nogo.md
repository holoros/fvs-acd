# v12.12 go/no-go: should the Nova Scotia data trigger a v12.12 release?

23 July 2026. Decision record. Question: does adding Nova Scotia (and the two open managed flags) justify
a v12.12 point release now, ahead of the v13.1 hierarchical refit? Verdict up front: NO-GO. Ship nothing as
v12.12; keep v12.10.1 as production, v12.11 as the finalized candidate, and fold Nova Scotia (especially the
long CFI record) into v13.1. This memo lays out the trigger, the criteria, the evidence on each, and the
fallback that would change the answer. No em dashes.

## What a v12.12 would even be for

Two, and only two, candidate motivations exist:
1. Ingest Nova Scotia (4,504 independent stand-pairs, PSP back to 1965) into the fitted model.
2. Patch the two named v12.11 managed flags: the Quebec MagPlot total-volume over-correction (basal area
   improves to -4.12% but volume worsens to +9.46%) and the SILC Seven Islands basal-area widening (+5.35%
   to +11.02%).

Everything else v12.11 set out to do is already done and validated, so there is no third reason on the table.

## Go/no-go criteria and the evidence

Criterion 1: Does the fix require only bounded changes to the existing gated levers (gate-off byte-identity
preserved)? PARTLY, and that is the problem. A management-gate retune (fmin/k/RD0) or the Lever 3b QMD gate
touches gate-ON behavior only, so byte-identical gate-off would survive. But the QC problem is that volume and
basal area must move in opposite directions there (pull volume down without giving back the basal-area gain),
and the existing levers are single global scalars applied to every managed stand. Tightening the gate to fix
QC volume regresses CTRN, NB, and Baxter, where the gate already works. QC is a source-specific deviation from
the pooled mean, which is exactly what a region or source random effect represents and what a global scalar
structurally cannot deliver. SILC is likewise a single-source basal-area effect. So the honest answer is that
a clean fix does NOT fit inside the existing lever structure. FAIL.

Criterion 2: Does ingesting Nova Scotia improve the model without cost? NO. Nova Scotia is currently the one
clean, fully independent generalization test in the whole benchmark, never used to fit or tune any lever or
the ESI anchor. That is precisely why the "managed levers generalize to NS" result carries weight. Fitting a
v12.12 to NS spends that asset and leaves nothing independent to check generalization against. The correct use
of NS as training data is inside a model built to be re-validated elsewhere, which is v13.1. FAIL as a v12.12
motivation.

Criterion 3: Is there operational urgency that cannot wait for v13.1? NO. v12.10.1 remains the shipped
production model and is unaffected. The two flags are disclosed, named v13.1 targets in the report, GTR, and
deck. Nobody is running an unflagged broken model. FAIL (no urgency).

Criterion 4: Is the cost of a v12.12 small? NO. Any change to a gated lever reopens the full validation stack
that v12.11 had to pass: byte-identical gate-off, the multi-source maturity gradient with Baxter held out, the
all-sites ME/NB/QC comparison against base and OSM, the Bakuzis five relations, the long-horizon plateau, and
a red-team pass. That is close to a full v13.1-scale validation cycle for a patch v13.1 will supersede in the
same era. And a rushed gate change under time pressure is exactly the setup that produced the string-vs-integer
disturbance-screening bug caught in this cycle. FAIL (cost is high, not low).

Criterion 5: Does v13.1 actually address these items better? YES. v13.1 is a hierarchical mortality and
ingrowth refit whose region and source random effects can decouple QC's volume from its basal area against the
pooled mean (the thing a global scalar cannot), can bring the developing-end recruitment in at subplot grain,
and is the designed vehicle to fold Nova Scotia, especially its long CFI record, into both fitting and the
benchmark set. It is already running.

## Verdict

NO-GO on v12.12. Four of five criteria fail; the fifth confirms v13.1 is the right vehicle. Fixing QC and SILC
requires the hierarchical structure v13.1 provides, ingesting Nova Scotia into a v12.12 would spend the one
clean independent test for no durable gain, there is no operational urgency because v12.10.1 ships, and the
validation cost of a gate change is near-full for a patch v13.1 supersedes. Recommendation on record and
unchanged: no v12.12; carry Nova Scotia into v13.1.

## The one condition that would change the answer

If v13.1 slips badly on convergence or timeline (its Bayesian fit has timed out before and chains run multiple
days), and the QC +9.46% volume over-correction becomes a live operational complaint from a Quebec user, then
the honest fallback is NOT a full v12.12 but a narrow, gate-ON-only adjustment to the QC volume-to-basal-area
ratio that leaves gate-off byte-identical and is re-validated on Nova Scotia as a second independent test
before release. Only under those two conditions together, and even then as a scoped v12.11.x patch, not a
v12.12 by default.
