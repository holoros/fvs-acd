# FVS-ACD v13.1, pass 3 handoff, 29 July 2026

Aggregates only. No coordinates left the node. No em dashes.

## Headline

The v13.1 scientific story inverted this pass. Two demographic levers were built, fitted, debugged
through three generations, and scored, and both are null. An independent tree-list decomposition then
localized the real defect, and an adversarial red-team pass broke the first statement of that
localization and forced a sharper and better-supported one. The developing-plot volume underprediction
is a failure to move surviving stems out of the 6 to 10 cm diameter classes, not a demography failure
and not a general diameter growth failure.

Two benchmark anchors were also found not to measure what they were believed to measure, one of which
turned out to be a data staging bug rather than a real limitation.

## What the failure actually is

An independent tree-list decomposition, run on a predicted t2 tree list that is byte-identical to the
base run at 0.000e+00 across all 35 numeric columns, partitions the 16.53 pp bias as follows.

| counterfactual | m3 ha-1 | bias % |
|---|---|---|
| predicted, actual | 137.343 | -16.53 |
| predicted diameters with observed height curve | 137.736 | -16.29 |
| observed diameters with predicted height curve | 162.743 | -1.09 |
| observed, actual | 164.544 | 0 |

Height explains +0.79 pp. The diameter distribution explains +15.98 pp. Interaction is -0.57 pp. The
distribution outweighs height by roughly 20 to 1, and of the 7.32 pp form-factor residual the
distribution explains 96.4 percent.

Total stem count is unbiased at +1.5 percent while the distribution is displaced downward. Share of
basal area below 12.7 cm runs 28.6 percent at t1, 27.7 percent observed at t2, and 36.8 percent
predicted. The real stand held its small-stem share flat across 20.5 years and the model raised it by
8 pp. The pile-up is survivors, not ingrowth: in the 6 cm class the surplus is 753 of 824 stems ha-1
from survivors against 71 from ingrowth, and ingrowth overall is 2.4 percent of predicted basal area
with a maximum diameter of 21.4 cm. Stem advance above 12.7 cm is 37.8 percent of observed, and above
25 cm, where stems cannot have arrived by ingrowth within the interval, the model delivers 46 percent
of observed accretion.

Suspected component: ddbh() at line 1015 of AcadianGY_13.1.r. Its size term is b1*log(dbh) + b2*dbh
with b1 = 0.37698 and b2 = -0.02568, so the elasticity b1 + b2*dbh crosses zero at 14.7 cm and goes
increasingly negative above it, imposing an interior maximum exactly where the model loses ground.
This hypothesis is stated but NOT yet tested; that is the top open item.

## What the red team broke, and what survived

The first statement of the localization claimed a diameter increment failure on the evidence that the
predicted QMD increment was 43 percent of observed. That claim is broken. QMD is exactly
sqrt((BA/TPH)/(pi/40000)), verified to a maximum deviation of 7.1e-15 cm across all 918 plots, and the
results file contains no TPH column at all so the reported TPH is itself back-derived from QMD. The
three structural numbers have two degrees of freedom, and "TPH unbiased, QMD low" is one fact stated
twice. The -56.5 percent increment figure is the -6.56 percent level figure divided by a small base,
and the original claim quoted QMD on an increment basis while quoting basal area and volume on a level
basis, which rigs the comparison. Drop "43 percent of observed" entirely.

What survives is stronger, because it is independent of the QMD identity. Forcing predicted basal area
to exactly equal observed by rescaling every expansion factor by 1.1089 still leaves volume at
-7.44 percent. The basal-area-weighted diameter distribution is invariant to uniform expansion
rescaling, and the predicted BA-weighted median diameter is 2.66 cm short (observed D25/D50/D75
11.94/20.07/30.99, predicted 9.76/17.41/29.35). Top height is unbiased at -0.13 percent on a level
basis and -0.91 percent on an increment basis, and heights are 99.94 percent measured in this extract,
so top height does not pass through the identity either.

The red team also confirmed that the deficit above 12.7 cm is flat rather than progressive, -16.9
percent in the 12.7 to 16 cm class and -17.4 percent above 40 cm, and that above the merchantable
threshold dBA and dTPH track each other to within a percentage point while dQMD sits between +0.29 and
+2.79. A general diameter growth deficit acting on large trees would deepen with size and does not.
So the defensible statement is that the failure localizes to the transition out of the small classes,
which relocates the problem without exonerating the increment function.

## Benchmark integrity findings

The harness scores each source by applying the same nsvb() emulator to observed and predicted tree
lists, and nsvb() silently substitutes a crude DBH-only curve himp() for any tree lacking a height.
Measured-height coverage varies enormously across sources and correlates with the scorecard.

PEF genuinely lacks heights. Its source tree file has no height column at all, heights live in a
separate file with 9,412 positive records against 589,718 tree records, 1.58 percent study-wide, and
the staged 4.02 percent over-delivers because heights were subsampled on larger trees. Rescoring PEF
with a Chapman-Richards curve fitted to 44,582 measured Maine FIA heights moves it from +1.24 to -3.83.
PEF therefore stops being evidence that the growth engine is unbiased on developing stands.

Baxter is a staging bug and must not be published as it stands. BaxterCFI_raw.csv carries predHT as a
provenance flag, 733 rows measured and 15,176 imputed, confirmed by decimal signature, with measured
counts by year of 2008: 276 and 2009: 310, exactly the benchmark t2 years. Joining back to the staged
t2 list gives 447 measured trees carrying 9.91 percent of t2 basal area, higher coverage than PEF,
and the staged extract reports 0.00 percent. The builder never selected the column. No
build_baxter_bench.R could be located although every sibling builder exists. The proposed Baxter
rescore from +9.09 to +3.89 was computed by substituting a modelled curve into a tree list that had
silently discarded the site's own measured heights, and a reviewer who opens the raw file finds them
in one line. Fix the staging, validate the curve against the 447, then rescore.

The himp defect is a hardwood defect, not a uniform one. Softwood error runs -0.4 percent at 4 to 6 cm,
-6.6 percent at 12 to 14 cm, and turns positive above 22 cm reaching +6.5 percent at 36 to 38 cm.
Hardwood runs -41.5 percent at 4 to 6 cm, -32.6 percent at 12 to 14 cm, and -8.8 percent above 40 cm.
Any claim that himp is uniformly 15 to 18 percent short is wrong. Note also that the Maine curve must
never be exported to Nova Scotia: it runs 16 percent tall against 233,000 measured NS heights.

The model's own height imputation is good and does not use himp. For null-height t1 trees it lands
within a few percent of what measured peers in the same class actually measured, and beats himp by more
than 30 percent on hardwood. The recommendation is NOT to run a t1 microplot height refill; expected
effect is under 0.6 pp.

## Mortality posterior, resolved after three attempts

| attempt | job | config | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12554985 | adapt_delta 0.99, treedepth 15, 4 chains, 72 h | TIMEOUT, 0 divergent but 100 percent at treedepth cap, multimodal |
| 2 | 12863545 | adapt_delta 0.92, treedepth 12, 6 chains | 3 h 27 m, Rhat 4.73, 780 divergent, chains frozen not converged |
| 3 | 12894600 | dropped free (1 given region), Intercept prior normal(-4.5, 0.5), warmup 2000 | 8 h 39 m, USABLE |

Attempt 3 diagnosis: dropping the free region term removed the flat direction. All 6 chains concordant
on lp__ with within-chain sd 20 to 57 against attempt 2's 478,140, zero divergent transitions, and both
variance components identified at sd_fortype 0.5827 [0.5164, 0.6570] and sd_region:eco 0.6811 [0.6181,
0.7514]. The three slopes are pristine at Rhat 1.0007, 1.0002 and 1.0009 with bulk ESS 6169, 6322 and
6098.

It fails its formal gate on b_Intercept (Rhat 1.535, bulk ESS 10.9) and on treedepth saturation (65.3
percent against a 20 percent gate). That failure is on an unreachable quantity. b0 appears at exactly
two lines of AcadianGY_13.1.r, line 102 where it is parsed and line 135 where it is stored, and
.acd_hier_eta_dev at line 167 returns only b_rd*z_rd + b_lnqmd*z_lnq + b_ba*z_ba + u. Mortality calls
that same function at lines 4177 to 4178 and applies the result as a pure multiplier at line 4195.
Posterior correlations between the intercept and the slopes are 0.009, -0.012 and 0.020 pooled with a
maximum within-chain value of 0.065. The posterior is usable and the combined candidate ran on it.

Science from the mortality fit, reproducible across two independent runs at different sampler settings
to within 0.1 posterior SD: hazard falls 4.905 percent [-4.957, -4.854] across relative density 0.10 to
0.80, against the subplot fit's roughly 11 percent and the v12.11 hand gate's roughly 86 percent
near-step at RD0 0.38. A one standard deviation increase in ln(QMD), a factor of 1.602 in QMD, raises
hazard 16.18 percent, a clean senescence signal. The caveat is that rd is marginal net of lnqmd and ba,
which covary. The conclusion is that the data want a gentle thresholdless density effect and there is
no support at either grain for the steep hand-tuned gate.

## Recruitment posterior, resolved after three generations plus a hook fix

Generation v5 bound the recruit expansion to stand density TPH2, median 1466, instead of the per-tree
microplot constant 185.243 ha-1, an 18 to 19x over-expansion. Because stand TPH scales roughly as
QMD^-2 this wrote a mechanical -2 ln QMD term into the response and gave lnqmd_z -2.368.

Generation v6 corrected the constant and converged cleanly but still failed PEF verification at a mean
predicted ingrowth of 33.60 against a base of 13.39. Root cause: the response was the microplot count
already multiplied by 185.243, so the negative binomial shape absorbed the inflation at 0.0559,
implying a variance to mean ratio near 1627, and at that shape the score weights collapse toward phi
over mu, making the fit do ratio matching rather than mean matching.

Generation v7 (job 12865188) models the raw integer microplot count with log(185.243) folded into the
offset. It passes every gate: max_Rhat 1.00853, 0 divergent, shape 2.1645 against a prefit glm.nb theta
of 1.7946, lnqmd_z -1.26708, and an n-weighted pred over obs calibration ratio of 1.00221 with
per-region ratios 0.864 to 1.081.

A separate hook defect then surfaced and was fixed. The multiplicative modifier was designed to equal
1.0 at training-mean covariates in order to preserve the engine's calibrated magnitude. That is true
pointwise and false in aggregate: a log-link modifier centered at the mean covariate inflates a
population mean by exp(sigma^2 / 2). Measured on the fitting data, eta_dev has sd 1.1979 and
mean(exp(eta_dev)) is 1.9703 over 207,109 rows, a 97 percent inflation present in v5 and v6 too and
masked by the larger response-scale error. An aggregate normalizer N = 1.9702779425 was added to
.acd_hier_load, shipped as v131r_ingrowth_norm.csv. Gate-off byte identity was re-proven at 0.000e+00
on both seed stands against the edited file, log gateoff_ingnorm_12895217.out. PEF then passed at ING_p
mean 12.623 against base 13.394 with TOTVOL +1.231 against base +1.242 and BA -6.337 against -6.326.

Hurdle and zero inflation are ruled out and should not be revisited. Reconstructing the fitted negative
binomial over all training rows gives an observed to predicted zero ratio of 0.998 with the whole count
distribution matching to the third decimal, and the engine already implements the two-process structure
natively via prob_ingrowth returning ingrowth.prob times exp(eta).

## Open items, ranked

1. Test the ddbh 14.7 cm elasticity hypothesis. This is the top item and it is the whole v13.1 story
   now. Characterize the fitted increment surface across 2 to 60 cm by species and competition, compare
   against the class-transition behaviour implied by the observed t1 to t2 distributions, and estimate
   what a corrected increment buys. The ceiling is known: substituting the observed diameter
   distribution closes the bias from -16.53 to -1.09.
2. Assess collateral before any increment change. The same function serves every source. Baxter is a
   mature held-out benchmark that OVERpredicts at +9.09, and PEF is near zero. A change that repairs
   Maine FIA while blowing out Baxter and PEF is not a fix. The evidence points toward a size-dependent
   or competition-dependent correction rather than a level shift.
3. Fix the Baxter staging bug, validate the curve against the 447 measured stems, rescore. Do not
   publish +3.89 until this is done.
4. Finish the combined candidate scorecard. Array 12959911 had 9 of 11 sources complete at handoff;
   mefia_subp and ns_long_unmanaged were still running.
5. Rebuild the Maine FIA tree linkage from ME_TREE.csv with PREV_TRE_CN carried through. The staged
   benchmark keys do not link across occasions: 28.3 percent of key-matched trees have negative DBH
   change with a 1st percentile of -26.4 cm, and 8,393 unmatched t2 trees are at or above 12.7 cm on
   plots that exist in the init file. This blocks tree-level increment validation, which is the
   definitive test of item 1, and it means observed ingrowth cannot be separated from observed mortality
   in this benchmark.
6. Cap the unbounded low-QMD recruitment tail. Recruitment scales as QMD^-2.68 with no bound; NB has
   16.1 percent of stands above 200 trees ha-1 yr-1 with a maximum of 3127 against a median of 4.55.
   Score effect is at most 1.3 pp so this is a credibility fix, not a lever. The cap must go inside
   .acd_hier_eta_dev UPSTREAM of the normalizer, and the normalizer must then be recomputed with the
   cap active.
7. Add a Jensen normalizer to the mortality hook. It currently has none, so it is pointwise-neutral but
   not aggregate-neutral, the same bug class just fixed on the recruitment side.
8. Deliverables rewrite. The 28 July plan was written before the story inverted and is now stale in its
   framing, not just its numbers.

## Decisions for Aaron

1. Ratify the ingrowth hook change from absolute replacement to multiplicative modifier, plus the

2. Confirm the v13.1 narrative shift. The mortality and recruitment posteriors are scientifically

   current formula has no management fixed effect. Recommended: defer to v13.2.

## Standing warnings

Do NOT enable lever 3a, the Reineke rein modifier at lines 1399 to 1410, against Maine FIA whole-plot.
With defaults d0 = 25 and floor 0.15 it suppresses increment for exactly the trees above 25 cm that are
already 46 to 57 percent short on accretion.

Provenance trap in the results filenames. The headline comes from v131base_mefia_l_results.csv. The
similarly named base_mefia_l_results.csv gives BA -9.93, QMD -6.48, TOPHT -0.53, TPH +1.16, and
cand_mefia_l_results.csv gives BA -7.45, QMD -11.91, TPH +10.12. Re-deriving from the wrong prefix
reproduces a plausible but wrong table. BASE_PREFIX is always v131base.

The ingrowth normalizer guarantees pointwise reproducibility across scoring-set size, which is what it
was designed for, but the mean modifier still differs by set composition (1.0796 against 2.1433 on two
test sets). State that explicitly rather than leaving it as a passing unit check.

Benchmark representativeness. Observed basal area rises 20.05 to 30.30 m2 ha-1 and stem count 1946.5 to
2460.6 trees ha-1 over 20.5 years, with every class above 6 cm gaining 30 to 70 percent in count. That
is not statewide Maine steady state; the 918-pair set looks selected toward undisturbed developing
stands and is testing growth vigour rather than representative dynamics.
