# FVS-ACD v12.11 candidate: red-team and stress-test report

*22 July 2026. Adversarial review of the v12.11 candidate ahead of the benchmark report, GTR, and deck. Read the
candidate report, the Lever 4 coefficient fit, the crown-ratio investigation, and the guardrail results, then
independently re-derived or stress-tested every claim rather than restating them. All work on Cardinal
(~/_scripts/AcadianGY_12.11.r), aggregates only; no edits made to the model file. New evidence this session:
an independent gate-off check on a novel stand, a 12-scenario stress battery (density, site, and composition
extremes, plus a lever-isolation trace) at density/site extremes and a 160-year horizon
(`redteam_stress_1211.R`, job 12521402; `redteam_topht_extreme.R`, job 12521523, both on Cardinal at
`~/magplot/verify/`), and a fresh read of the already-completed subplot re-score
(`cand_mefia_subp_results.csv` / `base_mefia_subp_results.csv`). No em dashes.*

## Verdict

**Finalize with fixes, not as-is.** The lever architecture, the gating discipline, and the byte-identical
gate-off guarantee are sound and independently reproduce (confirmed twice now, on two different stands). But
one blocker and several major findings undercut specific headline claims in the candidate and guardrail
reports and should not go into the GTR or deck unmodified. The blocker (the ESI dominant-height anchor
bypassing the tree height cap) is a cheap, mechanical fix. The majors reframe rather than reverse the
candidate's story: the top-height drift is not caused by any of the four gated levers, the developing-end
improvement does not survive at FIA subplot grain, Lever 2 can transiently overshoot the density ceiling in
already-overstocked managed stands, Lever 3b's stand constraint has no cohort exemption, and the *Quercus
rubra* L. (RO) height correction in Lever 4 is the largest and least-validated of the six species coefficients.
None of this is a case for reworking the lever concept; it is a case for fixing one mechanism, correcting three
claims, and documenting three watch items before the numbers go on a slide.

## Findings, ranked

### BLOCKER

**1. The ESI-anchored dominant-height mechanism bypasses the tree height cap and can produce non-monotonic,
biologically impossible top-height trajectories at long horizons on good sites.** `ops$use.esi.domht` (v12.7.0,
always ON in the guardrail/candidate harness via `USE_ANCHOR=TRUE`) recomputes a Chapman-Richards target
dominant height from CSI and stand age every year and rescales **every tree's height** on the plot by a factor
`cf = 1 + gain*(target/topht - 1)`, clamped to [0.7, 1.3] (AcadianGY_12.11.r line 3778-3801). This runs *after*
`calc_dht()`'s own height-cap enforcement (`(dht+ht) > max.height ~ 0`, line 1656-1660), and the anchor's
multiplicative rescale is never re-checked against `max.height`. I ran a 160-year projection of a mixed
RS/BF/YB stand at CSI 28 (a realistic, not extreme, Maine site index) with `max.height=50` set on every tree
(matching the exact default used in `bench_parity_split_ms_v1211.R` line 92 and 139). By year 160 the maximum
individual tree height reached 64.96 m, 30 percent over the stated 50 m cap, while the measured top-100 height
(topht) actually **declined** from 45.98 m at year 120 to 41.03 m at year 140 before recovering to 45.16 m at
year 160, a genuine oscillation, not noise, in what should be a monotonic non-decreasing stand metric. The
target/topht ratio (a proxy for how hard the anchor is having to work) sat at exactly 1.000 through year 100,
then rose to 1.279 at year 140, close to the clamp ceiling; across the full 160 years the ratio fell outside
the [0.7, 1.3] clamp band in 7 of 160 years, confirmed clamp saturation. No species in the Acadian region
approaches 60+ m; this is not an edge-of-plausibility result, it is a mechanism failure. **Fix:** re-apply
`pmin(max.height, ...)` after the anchor's rescale (a one-line change), and separately review whether the
Chapman-Richards target curve itself is well-behaved at CSI values above roughly 20-22 and ages above 100 to
120 years, since the target itself reached 56.35 m at CSI 28/age 160, which is the root cause pushing the
anchor to try to force an impossible correction in the first place. This is pre-existing (v12.7.0), not
introduced by v12.11, but it is always active in every reported v12.11 number and is directly implicated in
known issue (a). Fix before finalizing; it is small and the current guardrail battery never exercised a
horizon or site index long/high enough to catch it (guardrail 3's plateau test used CSI 18 and an all-softwood
stand; the in-domain FIA guardrail is short-interval, not 160-year).

### MAJOR

**2. The reported top-height drift (+4.21 to +5.88 percent) is not caused by any of the four gated levers; the
anchor makes lever identity irrelevant to top height whenever the clamp isn't saturated.** I ran the same
60-year mixed managed stand (CSI 18, RS/BF/SM/YB, form B/risk R3) through six configurations: anchor alone,
anchor plus recruitment, plus mortality gate, plus stand constraint, plus Lever 4, and all four candidate
levers together. Basal area at year 60 varied meaningfully across configurations (22.37 to 21.64 m² ha⁻¹,
about 3.4 percent range, confirming the levers really do change tree-level dynamics), but topht at year 60 was
**identical to three decimals, 20.269 m, in all six configurations.** This is because the anchor's target is a
function of CSI and age only; as long as the clamp does not bind, it forces top height onto the same curve
regardless of what ddbh, dht, or mortality do underneath it. The candidate report's watch item ("likely an
interaction of Lever 4 or the recruitment change with the dominant-height anchor") is therefore the wrong
lead. The actual driver has to be something in how the guardrail harness assembles the scored population, most
likely the `esi.domht.afac` calibration (NB_AFAC=0.579, QC_AFAC=0.111, applied per province) interacting with
how the candidate's "mixed managed/unmanaged" province split (`cand_mixed` = NB rows from the managed=TRUE
run, QC rows from the managed=FALSE run) differs from a single-pass base run, or a shift in which stands or
ages populate the in-domain set once ingrowth/mortality change stand composition. **Fix:** re-run guardrail 1's
base config through the identical `cand_mixed` province-assembly procedure (not a single-pass base) to isolate
harness-assembly effects from lever effects, and audit the `esi.domht.afac` assignment logic for asymmetries
between the managed=TRUE and managed=FALSE passes. Reframe the GTR language from "trace to Lever 4" to "traced
to the anchor calibration, not the new growth levers" once confirmed.

**3. Lever 2's management-gated mortality attenuation permits a large transient density overshoot in
already-overstocked managed stands, exactly the use case most likely to be run through this model.** On an
identical, deliberately overstocked initial stand (80 RS/BF trees, dbh 18 to 34 cm, 1600 stems ha⁻¹, roughly
2 to 3x a normal ceiling), the full candidate stack with `stand.managed=TRUE` peaked at 47.88 m² ha⁻¹ basal
area during the 160-year run before the hard SDImax ceiling pulled it back to a tail mean of 25.13. The same
stand with `stand.managed=FALSE` (natural self-thinning, no attenuation) peaked at only 30.65 m² ha⁻¹, and the
frozen base model (no candidate levers at all) peaked at 22.87. The managed-gated overshoot is roughly 33
percent above the model's normal ~36 m² ha⁻¹ self-thinning plateau (per guardrail 3) and roughly 56 percent
above the natural-gated trajectory's own peak on the identical starting condition. This isolates cleanly to
Lever 2: same stand, same recruitment and stand-constraint settings, only the managed flag differs. The
`mort.dens.scale` fmin=0.13 floor lets a "managed" stand's self-thinning removal fraction drop to 13 percent of
normal for years at a stretch, relying entirely on the separate hard SDImax ceiling (`sdm_keepc`) to eventually
correct it rather than tracking the self-thinning line the way the natural/base configuration does. No NaN,
negative, or genuinely runaway behavior resulted (the hard ceiling does eventually catch it, confirming that
specific safety rail works), but a user projecting a neglected, overdue-for-thinning managed stand, precisely
the stand type this lever was built to correct for, would see a multi-decade transient over-prediction of
standing volume before the model self-corrects. **Fix:** either raise fmin for stands that start well above
onset/break at initialization, or add an explicit check that the attenuated mortality fraction cannot let RD
climb materially above 1.0 between hard-ceiling corrections (a rate limiter rather than the current every-step
hard clamp), and add this scenario (overstocked managed start) to the guardrail battery going forward.

**4. The subplot-grain re-score, already completed on Cardinal, shows the developing-end recruitment fix
essentially does not transfer to subplot scale, and this undercuts the "back to parity" headline.**
`cand_mefia_subp_results.csv` and `base_mefia_subp_results.csv` (n=2858, already built per task history, not
"completing on the node" as the candidate report states) give total-volume bias of -24.90 percent (base) versus
-24.82 percent (candidate), an improvement of 0.08 percentage points, against the whole-plot improvement of
0.5 points (-16.6 to -16.1) that the candidate report leads with. Basal area does move somewhat at subplot
grain (-18.37 to -16.86 percent, 1.5 points), so the asymmetry between BA and total volume at subplot scale is
itself worth a look (total volume is more height-sensitive via NSVB, and topht at very small subplots with few
trees is a noisier statistic). This is exactly the concern known issue (b) raises: a flat multiplicative
ingrowth correction, tuned and validated at whole-plot aggregation, essentially disappears at the FIA subplot
(24 ft radius, about 0.017 ha) grain where recruitment is a highly patchy, clustered spatial process that a
stand-level multiplier cannot represent. **Fix:** correct the candidate report's characterization (the file
exists and the result is unfavorable, not pending), and either present the subplot result honestly as a caveat
in the GTR/deck ("the recruitment lever closes the developing-end gap at stand/plot aggregation but not at
subplot grain, consistent with recruitment being a spatially patchy process") or invest in a spatially explicit
recruitment treatment before claiming the developing end is fixed.

**5. Lever 3b's stand-constraint reallocation has no cohort or size-class exemption, and the QMD floor gates on
whole-stand structure, which can throttle vigorous small trees inside an otherwise mature stand.** Once
`ba.plot > k*eff_hi` (now reached far more readily since the lowered thresholds activate at QMD ≥ 22, versus
the un-gated defaults' 0.8*k), *every* tree's ddbh on the plot is replaced by
`sqrt((dba.tree.plot+ba)/k/expf) - dbh`, a value driven purely by that tree's share of the pre-constraint
predicted increment, rescaled to hit the stand BA-increment target (AcadianGY_12.11.r lines 1281-1299). This
preserves relative rank among trees but applies the same proportional derate to every tree regardless of size
class, which is exactly the Weiskittel et al. (2011) Chapter 10 caution the task brief flags: a stand-level
constraint with no cohort exemption will also rein in a vigorous regenerating or released understory cohort
inside a naturally two-aged or recently thinned "mature" (QMD ≥ 22) stand, not just the large trees causing the
overshoot. The QMD floor gates on the *whole-stand* quadratic mean diameter, so a stand with a scattered large
overstory and abundant regeneration below can cross the floor and get full-strength, stand-wide throttling.
This has not been tested against a genuinely two-cohort synthetic stand; the guardrail and maturity-gradient
benchmarks are pooled-stand statistics that would not surface this. **Fix:** exempt trees below some DBH or
crown-class threshold from the reallocation pool (redistribute the stand target only among the cohort actually
driving the overshoot), or at minimum add a two-cohort synthetic stress stand to the guardrail battery.

**6. The largest and single least-validated Lever 4 species coefficient belongs to the commercially important
species, *Quercus rubra* L. (RO).** Direct evaluation of `dht_hw_mod()` on Cardinal confirms the report's own
numbers: worst-case (form B, high risk) multiplier is 0.7334 for RO, a 26.7 percent height derate, more than
double the two species with genuine field validation (*Betula alleghaniensis* Britton, YB, 0.8728; *Acer
saccharum* Marshall, SM, 0.8737, both about 12.6 to 12.7 percent). RO's coefficient (spp.coeff.risk = -0.1520)
rests entirely on the FIA GRM damage-code proxy, the same proxy the Lever 4 report explicitly says the Smith
field NHRI dataset was adopted to avoid ("so no FIA damage proxy is needed"), because RO was not present in the
Smith height set (n=1, dropped). This is an internal inconsistency worth naming plainly: the report frames the
field data as the reason the correction is trustworthy, then falls back to the very proxy it set out to avoid
for the one species with the largest fitted effect. **Fix:** either source independent RO field height data
before shipping (Castle 2017 Master form/risk, joined to HRF_INVENTORY heights, is already flagged in the
report as the natural next step) or cap RO's coefficient conservatively (e.g., at the SM/YB magnitude) pending
validation, and say so explicitly in the GTR.

### MINOR

**7. The candidate ships the flat `ingrowth.mult=2.5` rather than the density-gated `use.ingrowth.rdtaper`
Lever 1 itself built and documents as protecting mature closed stands from the flat multiplier.** No
`V1211_IGTAPER` is set anywhere in the guardrail configuration (confirmed by grep of
`bench_parity_split_ms_v1211.R`); only the flat multiplier is used. This is not as serious as it first appears
because a separate, always-on density-release logistic (`ops$use.ingrowth.release`, default TRUE, center RD
0.58) already suppresses recruitment at high relative density regardless of which recruitment lever is active,
so the flat multiplier is not applied undamped in closed stands. But the report gives no reason for choosing
the flatter, less-targeted mechanism over the purpose-built taper, and the two have not been compared
head-to-head. Worth a one-line justification or a quick comparison before finalizing.

**8. Sub-2.5 cm dbh trees never receive an imputed height, so cr, ddbh, and dht all resolve to NA and freeze
permanently.** Confirmed by the crown-ratio investigation's own trace (tree 20 stuck at dbh 2.12 cm through 20
years) and by code inspection: the `ht` imputation case_when gates on `dbh>2.5` (line 3706). Practical exposure
in the shipped configuration is narrow, since model-created ingrowth enters at `ops$ingrowth.min.dbh=5.0`, well
above the gate; the real exposure is any externally supplied tree list carrying live stems in the roughly 2.0
to 2.54 cm dbh band (the edge of FIA's 1-inch sapling floor). When it happens, though, it fails silently:
no error, no warning, a permanently stalled tree that never grows and never dies from the base survival
function (which is dbh-driven, not cr-driven, so it survives indefinitely at zero growth). **Fix:** lower the
gate to the FIA sapling floor (dbh > 1.0 in / 2.54 cm) or give these trees an explicit small-tree height
estimate; cheap and it closes a real, if narrow, silent-failure mode exactly at the developing end Lever 1 is
trying to fix.

**9. Every reported Lever 4 guardrail number was scored against SPLIT-synthesized, not field-observed,
form/risk, and SPLIT applies an identical population-average multiplier to every stem of a species regardless
of that tree's actual condition.** `hw_formrisk_split.R` on Cardinal fractionally splits each hardwood tree
into multiple pseudo-records weighted by Smith Table A.9's marginal form and risk frequencies, combined via an
**assumed-independent** outer product (not the true joint distribution, which is not available), applied
identically to every tree of a species regardless of size, vigor, stand history, or condition; `dht_hw_mod()`
does not even use its `dbh` argument in the multiplier calculation. Because the split fractions and weights are
fixed per species, the expf-weighted average multiplier every SM stem receives in a SPLIT-scored benchmark is
deterministically the same number, worked out here as approximately 0.974 (a 2.6 percent height derate),
regardless of which individual tree it is. `SPLIT=TRUE` was used throughout the guardrail battery (per the
configuration table in the guardrail report) because that is what makes Lever 4 scoreable on benchmark sources
that lack a populated FVS "special" damage code. This means the elaborate NHRI risk-class framing does not
functionally differentiate individual trees anywhere in the reported validation; it is mathematically
equivalent, at scoring time, to a fixed per-species height derate. This is not necessarily wrong as an
operational approximation, but it should be described accurately (a species-average correction validated at
that level, not a tree-specific one) rather than as tree-level risk conditioning, and it also means the
guardrail improvement attributed partly to Lever 4 cannot be distinguished from "the model got a little shorter
per hardwood species" using the current benchmark. **Fix:** either hold out the four Smith source stands and
score Lever 4 against their real `special`-code form/risk (no SPLIT) as an independent check, or state the
species-average-equivalence explicitly in the GTR.

### COSMETIC

**10. The dht.hw.mod clamp [0.5, 1.2] is decorative under every currently fitted coefficient; it has never
been exercised end-to-end.** Direct evaluation confirms the worst real multiplier is 0.7334 (RO), and even a
hypothetical coefficient doubling (per the Lever 4 report's own caveat that an FIA-GRM increment refit "gave
slightly larger magnitudes") only reaches 0.5488, still inside the floor. Add a synthetic unit test with an
artificially large mock coefficient to confirm the clamp actually engages correctly, since it is the only
safety rail standing between a future refit and an unclamped multiplier, and it has not been proven to work.

**11. Potential future double-adjustment between the pre-existing diameter-side `ddbh_hw_mod` (Castle et al.
2018, gated `ops$use.hw.mod`) and the new height-side `dht_hw_mod` (Lever 4) has never been tested jointly.**
Confirmed `use.hw.mod` defaults FALSE and is not set TRUE anywhere in the guardrail harness
(`bench_parity_split_ms_v1211.R` line 93), so no double-counting occurs in any number reported so far. But the
two modifiers were fit independently (Castle 2018 diameter model; Smith field height model) and both suppress
growth for the same high-risk hardwood trees; the natural next operational step, correcting both diameter and
height for the same known problem, has not been validated to reproduce a sensible combined H:D allometry.
Flag for whoever turns `use.hw.mod` on alongside Lever 4 in a future config; not a defect in v12.11 as shipped.

**12. Process note, already self-resolved.** The guardrail report's own discovery of three non-agreeing
"in-domain" reference numbers in repo history, and its transparent resolution to the reproducible
BA -11.28/TOPHT +4.21/TOTVOL -4.41 figure, is good practice and does not need re-litigating here; just confirm
the cleanup lands in MEMORY.md and the affected handoff files as already flagged in that report.

## Independent verification performed this session

Byte-identical gate-off reconfirmed on a novel stand not used in any prior smoke test (hardwood-heavy
RS/SM/YB/RM mix, CSI 20, 30-year projection): max|ddbh| = max|dht| = max|dexpf| = 0.000e+00, no row-count
difference. Density extremes (very low density, and a deliberately overstocked near-3x-SDImax stand),
site extremes (CSI 6 and CSI 28), and composition extremes (pure softwood, pure worst-case and pure best-case
hardwood) were run 160 years under the full candidate stack with no NaN, negative diameter, or negative
expansion factor at any step; the only pathological behavior found was the transient density overshoot
(finding 3) and the anchor/cap interaction (finding 1), both reported above. The rein floor (0.15) is not
relevant to the shipped candidate, which uses the 3b stand constraint, not the 3a rein.
