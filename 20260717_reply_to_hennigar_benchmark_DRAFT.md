Chris,

Thanks, and I am with you completely on the bias point. That is exactly why I leaned on the AI-assisted
workflow for this, it does not care whose model it is, and it caught one of my own analysis errors along
the way, which was humbling and useful. Letting the data referee is the only way I know to keep myself
honest, and OSM coming out as strong as it does is part of that story, not something I am going to talk
around.

Since we last traded notes I have pushed the independent data a good deal wider. Beyond the MagPlot New
Brunswick and Quebec pairs I have folded in the Maine FIA remeasurements, both the roughly twelve thousand
short-interval pairs and a clean first-to-last set on the undisturbed plots that reaches twenty years, plus
the Penobscot Experimental Forest compartment study, the CFRU commercial thinning trials, and the Seven
Islands CFI, all scored on the same NSVB currency. The encouraging part is that FVS-ACD actually holds up
better on that independent Maine and research-forest data than on the QC and NB set it was tuned against,
which probably says as much about how hard those QC and NB pairs are as it does about the model.

The one thing that jumped out, and that I think is a shared growth and yield problem rather than any one
model's, is a systematic volume under-prediction on the long twenty-year FIA intervals, on the order of
fifteen to twenty percent, that simply does not show on the short intervals. That feels like a good concrete
thing to put in front of the group on the 18th, and I would bet OSM shows something similar over that
horizon. Worth looking at side by side.

I will get you the updated benchmarking write-up before the meeting. Have a good weekend, and here is hoping
August takes its time.
