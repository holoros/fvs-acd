# Red-team of the shipped FVS-ACD v12.10.1

*4 July 2026. Fresh adversarial pass on the shipped operational build FVS-ACD v12.10.1 (robust additive-release
management modifiers, gate on), run as the ORIENT stage of a stress-and-parity cycle for the 18 August Hennigar
meeting. Each challenge is attacked as a skeptical reviewer would, then resolved or documented against source
numbers regenerated this run. All computation on Cardinal (~/magplot/verify); aggregates only, coordinates never
left the node. Plaintext math, no em dashes.*

## Verdict up front

v12.10.1 survives the red team. The managed-cell win over OSM is real, out of sample, and physically bounded.
The one genuinely new item this pass surfaces is not a defect in the management lever but an honesty gap in the
biological-realism story: recruitment is under-predicted against the observed MagPlot ingrowth rates, which is
not yet stated in the deck. Fold it in as a disclosed limit. Nothing here changes the shipped-version decision:
v12.10.1 stays operational; the pooled gap to OSM is real and honestly framed.

Six challenges, outcomes:

1. Managed win robust, not a 146-stand artifact. Confirmed.
2. Additive-release validity (SDImax, negative implied mortality, self-thinning cap). Confirmed physical.
3. Consistency with the v13.1 growth-not-mortality finding. Confirmed, mutually reinforcing.
4. Pooled honesty (OSM still ahead pooled and unmanaged). Confirmed and correctly framed.
5. Equivalence and Bakuzis not gamed. Confirmed.
6. Deck and email overclaim. No overclaim of the management lever; one omission (ingrowth) to add.

## Challenge 1: is the managed win an out-of-sample result or a 146-stand tuning artifact?

Attack. The additive release is fit on 51 planted and 95 PCT MagPlot stands and scored on the same domain. Does
the gap closure to OSM survive cross validation, or is it in-sample flattery?

Finding, from results/mgmt/mgmt_gap_closure_robust.csv regenerated this run. Leave-one-installation-out (fold
unit = MagPlot installation), mean absolute level bias across the nine NSVB metrics:

| class | n | v12.9.x | v12.10.1 out of sample | OSM | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PLANTED | 51 | 15.77 | 3.95 | 16.69 | v12.10.1 clearly ahead |
| PCT | 95 | 7.48 | 3.04 | 3.73 | v12.10.1 ahead in core |
| MANAGED | 146 | 10.18 | 1.33 | 8.33 | gap closed, v12.10.1 ahead |

The managed number is out of sample (1.33 versus OSM 8.33), and it is not a clamped constant that cannot move:
the additive fold coefficients vary with the data (planted basal-area release 3.57 to 4.14 across folds, PCT 2.28
to 2.57), the specific weakness the previous red team flagged in v12.10.0. Leave-one-province-out holds managed
at 1.5 and planted at 5.6 (OSM 16.7); the 200-rep managed split-half is 5.6, beating OSM in 76 percent of reps
and v12.9.x in 82 percent. The win is real and it transfers. Resolved.

## Challenge 2: does the additive release produce non-physical stands?

Attack. Adding basal area to managed stands could push them past SDImax, or the release could imply negative
mortality, or it could fight the self-thinning cap.

Finding. Predicted stand density index for managed stands stays inside the envelope: planted predicted SDI mean
405, max 803; PCT mean 399, max 1276; unmanaged mean 449, max 1135. The planted release does not create the
densest stands in the set, it lifts under-stocked planted stands toward observed. Nothing is pushed to an extreme
relative to the unmanaged distribution. On mortality, the release is a diameter-growth term, not a mortality
term, so it cannot imply negative mortality; the base survival equations are untouched and the release is fit on
observed minus predicted-untreated increment (direct effect only). On the self-thinning cap: the 160-year plateau
sweep holds at every amplitude tested (basal area at 160 years 36.3, 41.6, 43.6, 45.6, 47.3 m2 ha-1 at amplitudes
1.0, 1.6, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, standard deviation over the last 20 years about 0.21, flat). The ceiling rises with
amplitude because basal area at the Reineke limit rises with tree size, which is correct, and the trajectory
still plateaus rather than running away. The release accelerates the approach and lifts the ceiling; it does not
break the cap. Resolved, physically bounded.

## Challenge 3: is v12.10.1 consistent with the v13.1 finding that management acts through growth, not mortality?

Attack. The v13.1 kickoff concluded, on 8.22 M independent FIA remeasurement trees, that the management signal is
a growth-release effect and that a mortality management term is not warranted (the mortality hazard fit does not
converge across four brms parameterizations and its one clean reference leaves both hazard intervals spanning no
effect). Does v12.10.1 contradict this?

Finding. It agrees, and the two are mutually reinforcing. v12.10.1 carries management as a bounded diameter-growth
amplitude on planted and PCT stands, not as a mortality modifier (mortality is deliberately left on the untreated
logistic to avoid distorting the asymptote, Ch 11). The v13.1 growth-scale fit independently identifies the same
effect: planted survivor diameter increment +0.087 cm yr-1 (t 26) against an unmanaged base of about 0.118, a
roughly 1.7 multiplier, and net basal-area increment planted +0.290 m2 ha-1 yr-1, about +2.9 m2 ha-1 over a
ten-year interval, the same order as the v12.10.1 MagPlot additive planted release of +3.8 m2 ha-1 from
independent data. The v12.10.1 lever is on the correct scale (growth), the correct direction (positive, strongest
planted), and the correct magnitude (above the old 1.6 clamp), exactly what the FIA-scale hierarchical fit finds.
Resolved: no contradiction; v12.10.1 is the interim engine expression of the v13.1 result.

## Challenge 4: pooled honesty, is OSM still ahead and is that stated?

Attack. Does v12.10.1 quietly claim parity it does not have on the pooled and unmanaged domain?

Finding, from scorecard_five_v1210.csv. Pooled across the 705 in-domain stands and nine metrics, OSM leads:
mean absolute level bias OSM 3.2 versus v12.10.1 5.0 percent, mean modeling efficiency OSM 0.75 versus 0.71, TOST
level equivalence OSM 8 of 9 versus 7 of 9. The pooled set is dominated by unmanaged stands, where v12.10.1 is
byte-identical to v12.9.x (NONE total-volume bias identical to the digit, +0.99 both, basal area -8.16 both), so
the pooled number is a v12.9.x number and OSM is ahead on it. This is stated correctly: the deck and brief frame
v12.10.1 as winning the managed cell and matching on quadratic mean diameter, biological realism, transparency,
and a climate-ready site driver, not as winning pooled precision. No pooled-parity overclaim exists. Closing that
pooled gap is the subject of the parity test in the companion memo. Resolved, honestly framed.

## Challenge 5: are the equivalence regions and the Bakuzis relations gamed?

Attack. Wide TOST regions or a lenient Bakuzis test could manufacture passes.

Finding. The TOST regions are the standard Robinson and Froese 2004 choices: level region plus or minus 10 percent
of the observed mean, increment region plus or minus 25 percent of observed periodic annual increment. They are
not inflated, and on them no model is broadly increment-equivalent, the honest signal that the remaining work is
on the increment. The Bakuzis relations are scored on the self-thinning-phase-aware test (Reineke slope fit only
where quadratic mean diameter is genuinely rising), which is stricter, not looser, than a naive whole-trajectory
slope: v12.9.x and v12.10.1 return Reineke slope -1.59 (target near -1.605), pass the plateau (basal area at 160
years 36), and pass site ordering, Eichhorn, and Sukachev. v12.5.0 still fails self-thinning outright (flat
Reineke, runaway basal area), which the matrix figure shows plainly. The tests are not gamed; the failures they
should catch, they catch. Resolved.

## Challenge 6: does the deck or the emails overclaim?

Attack. Any statement stronger than the numbers support.

Findings. On the management lever, no. The managed win is cross-validated and labeled as such, the PCT within-core
caveat is present, the Quebec edge is labeled out of domain, and the sample-size labels (pooled and TOST on 733,
management breakdown on 705) are distinct. The clamp weakness of v12.10.0 is mentioned only as resolved.

One omission, not an overclaim. The deck presents Bakuzis pass on all five relations and the plateau as the
biological-realism story but does not state the ingrowth result. Predicted recruitment under-predicts the
observed MagPlot rates (New Brunswick predicted 17.9 versus observed about 39.8 stems ha-1 yr-1, Quebec predicted
11.7 versus about 19.7), even though recruitment responds in the correct direction to density and management
(planted stands recruit more than unmanaged: New Brunswick planted 21.5 versus unmanaged 16.2). This is a real,
disclosed limit that belongs on the biological-realism slide, not a reason to change the version. It is quantified
in the companion stress-and-parity memo and added to the scorecard as an ingrowth row.
