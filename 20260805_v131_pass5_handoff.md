# FVS-ACD v13.1, pass 5 handoff, 5 August 2026

Aggregates only. No coordinates left the node. No em dashes.

## Headline

Pass 4 named b1 as the lever and produced two candidate refits. Pass 5 was supposed to choose between
them. It did, but the more important result is that the choice barely matters, because the increment
function is not where most of the bias lives. Three independent tracks converged on the same
accounting: the diameter increment reshaping is worth about 1.1 pp, the Chapter 10 constraint about
0.7 to 1.2 pp, a previously unscoped convexity gap about 3.9 pp, and the remaining 9.2 pp, roughly 60
percent of the available 15.44, is demographic and untouched by anything tested in passes 3 through 5.

The pass 4 ratification of r2 survives, but for a different reason than it was granted, and the
out-of-sample test that was supposed to confirm it turned out to be invalid.

## The convexity gap, new and the largest single piece found this pass

The model updates dbh = dbh + ddbh and expf = expf - dexpf at lines 4058 to 4064 and never removes
trees, so it propagates one deterministic trajectory per record and then scores a convex function of
the accumulated diameter. Volume goes roughly as D^2.5 and basal area as D^2, so E[v(D)] exceeds
v(E[D]) by an amount set by the within-cell variance of D. The model computes v(E[D]); the benchmark
observes E[v(D)]. No recalibration of b0, b1 or b2 on a conditional mean can remove this, because the
defect is in the second moment.

The measurement-error haircut, which was the main worry, turned out to be small. Total within-cell sd
of 20.5 year increment is 2.5886 cm against a measurement error sd of 0.2981 cm, so measurement error
is 1.3 percent of the variance and the haircut costs 0.11 pp. A global variance scalar is nearly as
good as a fitted variance function (3.986 against 3.866 pp), so the simple form is adequate.

Movement on mefia_l: base -16.531 to -12.664, or +3.866 pp. On top of r2, -11.586. The correction and
the increment reshaping are almost exactly additive: predicted -11.535 against realised -11.586, an
interaction of -0.051 pp. Every source moves in the same direction because a smearing correction on a
convex function always raises predicted volume, which is why the improvement is not by itself evidence
the correction is right.

## The pooled out-of-sample test failed, and the failure is instructive

The pooled Acadian refit was supposed to test whether r2 is too aggressive. It returned a required
surface of 1.23 to 1.25 across 8 to 25 cm, roughly four times r2's 0.98 to 1.06, which would have
meant r2 was far too conservative rather than too aggressive. **That result is an artifact and should
not be used.**

Leave-one-source-out, which two prior attempts failed to compute, settles it. Dropping any single
source moves the pooled surface by under 0.05 except one:

| dropped | b1 | surface at 8 / 16 / 25 / 40 cm |
|---|---|---|
| none (ALL) | 0.4823 | 1.231 / 1.249 / 1.225 / 1.153 |
| baxter | 0.4883 | 1.229 / 1.250 / 1.227 / 1.155 |
| **ctrn** | **0.1778** | **1.126 / 1.042 / 1.020 / 1.041** |
| mefia_l | 0.4366 | 1.253 / 1.257 / 1.237 / 1.184 |
| nb | 0.4492 | 1.241 / 1.246 / 1.222 / 1.160 |
| ns_managed | 0.7559 | 1.169 / 1.296 / 1.283 / 1.137 |
| ns_unmanaged | 0.5551 | 1.276 / 1.321 / 1.295 / 1.192 |
| pef | 0.4405 | 1.327 / 1.314 / 1.272 / 1.185 |
| silc_l | 0.4818 | 1.231 / 1.249 / 1.226 / 1.154 |

CTRN alone carries the entire pooled signal. Its required multiplier is 1.759 against 0.98 to 1.26 for
every other source. The cause is now clear: **CTRN's median remeasurement interval is 1.0 year**
against 5 to 20 years everywhere else, and the pooled fitting set drops all non-positive increments
(negative PAI is exactly 0.00 percent in every source, which is a filter signature, not a property of
the data). At a one year interval with a 0.1 inch recording quantum, measurement error is comparable
to true growth, and truncating the resulting distribution at zero inflates the mean substantially.
CTRN's median PAI of 0.260 cm per year is almost exactly the rounding quantum, which is the tell.

Excluding CTRN the pooled surface is 1.02 to 1.13, and Maine FIA alone on the decontaminated offset is
1.015. r2 at 0.98 to 1.06 sits inside that band, possibly a little conservative at the small end.
**The pooled test does not overturn r2, and as run it is invalid.** To be worth anything it must be
rerun with a minimum interval of five years and with non-positive increments retained rather than
dropped.

## The Chapter 10 constraint, diagnosed and repaired

The dimensional charge is confirmed and the argument is a priori rather than empirical. plot_ba_incr
returns dba = rt*BA*(1 - BA/k), the derivative of a logistic trajectory for standing basal area. What
must vanish at carrying capacity is dBA/dt, not gross accretion; trees do not stop growing at k,
stands stop accumulating. The code applies that net-scale target as a ceiling on the sum of gross tree
increments at lines 1419 to 1462 and calc_mortality then subtracts a drain from the already-capped
stand. The stand is debited twice against a target that budgeted for one debit.

Observed gross survivor basal area growth is flat to slightly rising with density (0.532 at BA 8.1 to
0.728 at BA 50.6 m2 ha-1 yr-1, slope +0.0053, p = 1.9e-6) while observed net falls hard (0.682 to
0.211, slope -0.0154, p = 1.9e-27). The Chapter 10 target is 50 to 78 percent of observed gross at
every density but 0.61 to 1.96 times observed net. A ceiling sitting below the central tendency of
what it caps is misspecified regardless of the gross versus net question.

One pass 4 number does not survive. The claim that the increment function over-predicts gross growth
at high density by a factor of two is wrong as stated. At projection year 1, where model basal area
equals observed t1 exactly, model over observed gross is 0.98 to 1.31 across strata, a roughly
constant 27 to 31 percent above BA 20, not a density-dependent doubling. The 1.41 figure is
manufactured by the projection through stem accumulation, not by the increment function.

The repair replaces the gross comparison with a net one, allocating allowed gross as dba plus the
expected drain. Gate-off byte identity holds at exactly 0.000e+00, including a self-test where the
reimplemented block reproduces the original bit for bit. In-band bind rate falls from 99.6 to 76.3
percent and shrink softens from 0.567 to 0.717 in the lower band. k = 64.45952 is marginally falsified
(observed t2 maximum 68.14 m2 ha-1 across sources), and **k = 70 is recommended**: it clears the
observed maximum, keeps the constraint a live guard, and values of 75 to 85 would place the upper
onset at or above the observed maximum, which is choosing k to disable the constraint rather than to
represent capacity.

Honest cost accounting: of the 0.79 pp the repair delivers on mefia_l, about 0.53 pp is raising k
alone and only 0.26 pp is the dimensional change. The dimensional fix as implemented triples runtime
because it adds a calc_mortality call per stand-year. Adopt it on correctness grounds, but
reimplemented against the stand-level stand_mort_ba predictor rather than the full tree-level call.

Separate finding: mefia_subp shows 4.13 percent of observed t2 basal area above k with a maximum of
130.24 m2 ha-1. A stand-level carrying capacity is not meaningful at subplot grain and the constraint
arguably should not be applied there at all.

## The selection target, closed

Settled from the model's own code rather than by assumption. Because trees are never removed and expf
is decremented, the stems represented at t2 are exactly the survivors carrying full-interval growth,
so unbiasedness requires the survivor increment conditional on surviving. Stems that died contribute
nothing to standing t2 volume and their partial-interval growth enters the estimator nowhere. The pass
4 worry that the correction might invert in sign is closed: it is upward above roughly 10 cm and
downward below.

Two pass 4 numbers are corrected rather than removed. The 0.9709 expansion-factor-weighted ratio is a
weighting artifact placing 49.7 percent of its mass on the 2.54 to 5 cm microplot class which carries
9.1 percent of standing volume; under every volume-relevant weighting the model under-predicts
survivor increment by 7 to 13 percent. The 18.9 percent of stems with no t2 record is not a linkage
failure in aggregate: compounding single-hop FIA survival to the mean span reproduces the observed
live share to within a percentage point, so at most about 1 pp can be reclassified.

## The new mature benchmark

Baxter is no longer a sample of one. A Maine FIA consecutive-pair mature stratum was built (PROV
ME_FIA_M, n = 1757, 93.3 percent of t2 basal area on measured heights, PPS-1 rejecting 0 of 384,981
records) with a matched control (n = 1759). Both under-predict at base, memat -7.98 against control
-7.33, so **maturity carries almost no signal and neither replicates Baxter**. The cut-sensitivity
grid covers all 28 combinations of LT30 and BA thresholds and base is negative at every single cut, so
the verdict does not depend on where the boundary was drawn.

Baxter is an outlier on level, by roughly 7 pp against a structurally matched mature set. Composition
and interval are both ruled out as explanations. Validating the height curve against Baxter's own 463
measured heights gives k = 1.065, moving its base from +8.77 to +2.99, which independently confirms
the pass 4 note that the Maine curve runs 6 percent short there. Read +2.99 to +8.77 as the honest
bracket, with the caveat that height sample trees are not a random subsample and the ratio may be
inflated by selection toward dominants.

## Standing warnings

BASE_PREFIX is always v131base. base_ and cand_ reproduce plausible but wrong tables.

Verify array completion by CSV row count, never by SLURM exit code.

Do not use QMD as evidence; it is exactly sqrt((BA/TPH)/(pi/40000)).

The Chapman-Richards height correction applies to PEF and regenerated Baxter ONLY, plus memat and
medev on their own site calibration. Applying it to all 11 moved ctrn -2.6 pp and nb -7.7 pp.

Do NOT enable lever 3a, the Reineke rein at lines 1399 to 1410, against Maine FIA whole-plot.

NEW: any tap of ddbh must record the RAW pre-constraint increment. The Chapter 10 constraint is
applied inside calc_ddbh, so recording its return contaminates the offset. On the dense subsample the
apparent required boost is 1.474 at 8 cm post-constraint against 0.897 pre-constraint.

NEW: the benchmark STAND_PLOT_TREE key is a per-plot 1..n sequence in BOTH the t1 and t2 files, so
matching on it pairs the k-th t1 stem with the k-th t2 stem and any survival or increment it implies
is meaningless. Baxter's staged TREE is a positional renumbering and a naive raw-tree-id join
misaligns DBH by up to 188 cm.

NEW: filtering a fitting set to positive increments is not innocuous. Combined with a short
remeasurement interval it produces severe upward truncation bias, which is what invalidated the pooled
refit. Retain non-positive increments or impose a minimum interval, preferably both.

NEW: R module version must match the library path. R/4.4.0 against the 4.5.2 personal library fails
with an undefined symbol on data.table.
